# Online SARIMA and seasonal Holt-Winters ETS against statsmodels: a numpy study (PLAN tasks 487 and 488)

*Status, 2026-11-21: a record, in progress for request A16. Part 1: online
SARIMA by the recursive prediction-error method at `s = 7` against
statsmodels' `SARIMAX`, fifty seeds (§3 to §5). Parts 2 to 4 (SARIMA at
`s = 24` and `168` and its state table; seasonal Holt-Winters ETS(A,A,A),
(A,Ad,A) and multiplicative seasonality against `ETSModel`; the tolerances)
follow. Nothing in the package or the Rust crates was changed. The scan this
builds on is `vm-tools/research/485-TIME-SERIES-SCAN.md` on branch
`vm-tools/oracle-gap-scan`, sections 3 and 4 ("scan §3", "scan §4"); the
earlier study it extends is `docs/records/ORACLE-STUDY-485.md` on branch

`scripts/study_487_488.py`.*

## 1. The questions

1. Does the recursive prediction-error estimator of study 485's ARMA(1,1),
   extended by a ring of `d + sD` lags for the differencing and by seasonal AR
   and MA terms, land within 2 of `SARIMAX`'s batch standard errors of
   `SARIMAX`'s batch fit on the same rows, on at least 45 of 50 seeds at 10,000
   rows, at `s = 7`, `24` and `168`?
2. At fixed parameters, does its recursion equal `SARIMAX`'s Kalman filter, and
   to how many ulps?
3. What does its state cost in bytes as a function of `s`?
4. The same for seasonal Holt-Winters (parts 3 and 4).

**The criterion** (study 485, kept): on each of fifty seeds
`485000` to `485049`, at 10,000 rows, every parameter of the online
estimate at the last row within 2 of the oracle's standard errors of the
oracle's batch estimate on all rows; the set passes when at least 45 of 50
seeds do.

## 3. The SARIMA estimator (487)

**The model** (statsmodels' signs):

```text
phi(B) Phi(B^s) w_t = theta(B) Theta(B^s) e_t,     w_t = (1 - B)^d (1 - B^s)^D y_t
phi(B)   = 1 - phi_1 B - ... - phi_p B^p           Phi(B^s)   = 1 - Phi_1 B^s - ...
theta(B) = 1 + theta_1 B + ... + theta_q B^q       Theta(B^s) = 1 + Theta_1 B^s + ...
A(B) = phi(B) Phi(B^s),   C(B) = theta(B) Theta(B^s),   delta(B) = (1 - B)^d (1 - B^s)^D
```

**Prediction** of row `t`, before `y_t` is read, from the rings (`ê` the kept
residuals):

```text
ŵ_t = - sum_{k>=1} A_k w_{t-k} + sum_{j>=1} C_j ê_{t-j}
ŷ_t = ŵ_t - sum_{k>=1} delta_k y_{t-k}
```

**The gradient** `psi_t = -de_t/dbeta`, with `e = A(B) w / C(B)`, is carried by
four filtered signals at the current parameters, each a ring (`x / C(B)` is
the recursion `x_t - sum_j C_j xf_{t-j}`):

| signal | definition | its gradient entries |
|---|---|---|
| `u` | `Phi(B^s) w_t / C(B)` | `psi_{phi_i} = u_{t-i}` |
| `v` | `phi(B) w_t / C(B)` | `psi_{Phi_i} = v_{t-s i}` |
| `r` | `Theta(B^s) e_t / C(B)` | `psi_{theta_j} = r_{t-j}` |
| `z` | `theta(B) e_t / C(B)` | `psi_{Theta_j} = z_{t-s j}` |

At fixed parameters and a zero start these are the exact derivatives of the
conditional sum of squares; a check holds them to a central difference of it
to 2.0e-11 (full spec) and 2.3e-11 (airline) relative (§5).

**The step** (Gauss-Newton, gain `1/W_t`, `W_t` the rows since the second
warm phase began):

```text
R_t    = R_{t-1} + (psi_t psi_t' - R_{t-1}) / W_t
beta_t = Proj(beta_{t-1} + R_t^{-1} psi_t e_t / W_t)
s2_t   = s2_{t-1} + (e_t^2 - s2_{t-1}) / W_t
```

`Proj` clips each coefficient to `[-0.99, 0.99]`: with each polynomial of
order at most one, the specs measured here, that is the stationary and
invertible region (a higher order needs the partial-autocorrelation transform
statsmodels uses, not measured). The kept residual is the a-posteriori one, at
the new parameters (study 485's choice for ARMA).

**The start**, with nothing buffered beyond the rings:

1. rows `0 .. d + sD - 1` fill the differencing ring and emit NaN, each with
   the stated reason "filling the differencing ring (`L` rows: d, D, s); no
   differenced value yet";
2. the next `warm = max(50, 2s)` rows run at `beta = 0` (they emit the
   seasonal-naive prediction of the differencing alone) and accumulate the
   lag-1 and lag-`s` autocorrelations of `w`, which start `phi_1` and `Phi_1`
   (at `phi = theta = 0` the two regressors are the same column and `R` is
   singular, study 485 §12);
3. the next `warm` rows build `R` and `s2` at held `beta`;
4. steps follow.

No batch start was needed at `s = 7` (§5).

## 4. The SARIMA oracle and its settings

`statsmodels.tsa.statespace.sarimax.SARIMAX`, statsmodels 0.15.0:

| setting | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| `order`, `seasonal_order` | `(p, d, q)`, `(P, D, Q, s)` of the spec | the same model |
| `trend` | `"n"` | the series have no drift after differencing |
| `simple_differencing` | `True` | the filter sees `w`, the same differenced rows the online estimator sees; the first `d + sD` rows are dropped, as the online estimator's ring drops them. With `False` the differencing enters the state (338 states at `s = 168` against 170) |
| `enforce_stationarity`, `enforce_invertibility` | `True`, `True` (the defaults) | the online projection keeps the same region |
| `concentrate_scale` | `False` (default) | `sigma2` is a parameter with its own SE |
| initialization | the default: stationary (Lyapunov) for the ARMA state | |
| `measurement_error`, `mle_regression`, `use_exact_diffuse` | defaults (`False`, `True`, `False`) | |
| `fit` | `method="lbfgs"`, `maxiter=200`, `cov_type="opg"`, `disp=False`; default start parameters | statsmodels' default method; `maxiter` raised from 50 so no fit stops at the cap (none came near it: at most 13 iterations) |
| standard errors | `res.bse` (outer product of gradients) | as study 485's ARMA |

The online estimator minimizes the conditional (zero-start) squared error;
`SARIMAX` maximizes the exact likelihood. The two share a limit.

**Fixed parameters.** At the batch parameters, the online recursion from a
zero start against `SARIMAX(...).filter(params).forecasts` on the same `w`:
the zero start's difference decays with the MA roots and is gone by row 500.
Over the last 1,000 rows:

- with statsmodels' default filter, the gap is about 1e-9 (median 7.8e-10 and
  1.1e-9, max 1.5e-9 and 1.8e-9 for the two specs): statsmodels stops updating
  the state covariance once it has converged (`ssm.tolerance = 1e-19`) and
  uses the converged gain from then on, which differs from the exact gain in
  the ninth digit;
- with that switch off (`model.ssm.tolerance = 0`, the filter then runs its
  full update on every row), the gap is at rounding: at most 2.2e-15 absolute
  on `|w|` up to 8.5, at most **3.5 ulps** of the largest `|w|` in the window.

So the class-E oracle for the fixed-parameter recursion is `SARIMAX.filter`
with `ssm.tolerance = 0`, after the zero start's transient; the default
filter can only be held to about 2e-9.

## 6. Part 2: `s = 24` and `s = 168`, and the state against `s`

### 6.1 Two changes to the harness, neither to the estimator or the oracle

**`SARIMAX`'s memory at `s = 168`.** The default `fit` ends with a smoother
pass whose results store `n r²` floats per covariance array: 2.3 GB each at
10,000 rows and 170 states. Four fits at once used the machine's 15 GB and the
kernel killed them. The fits now run `fit(low_memory=True, cov_type="none")`
and then `filter(params, cov_type="opg", conserve_memory=...)` with the flags
that drop the stored state covariances, gains, smoothing arrays and forecast
covariances but keep the likelihood and the forecast means. On four `s = 7`
fits this gives the default path's parameters, standard errors,
log-likelihood and forecasts **to the bit** (every difference 0.0), so §4's
settings stand. Part 1's grid ran the default path and was not rerun.

**A batch start beside the plain one.** At `s = 168` the plain start fails
(§6.3). Each case now also runs remedy (a) of study 485, against the same
`SARIMAX` fit:

- the first `W = max(500, 3s)` differenced rows (500 at `s = 24`, 504 at
  `s = 168`) are buffered and emit NaN, each with the stated reason
  "buffering W differenced rows for the batch start (fitted by the core's
  optimizer); no parameters yet";
- their conditional least squares (zero start) is maximized by study 485's
  core-held optimizer (`batch_core.maximize`: projected Newton with Armijo
  backtracking, the curvature from central differences of the analytic
  gradient), with the gradient and Gauss-Newton matrix from the estimator's
  own recursions at fixed parameters, the projection the online clip, and the
  start grid `{-0.5, 0, 0.5}` per coefficient (9 points for the airline
  spec, 81 for the full one);
- the buffer is replayed at the fitted parameters with them held, building
  `R`, `s2` and every ring; steps then follow at gain `1/W_t`, `W_t` counting
  the buffer. The warm phases of §3 are skipped.

The buffer is `8 W` bytes (4,000 B at `s ≤ 166`, `24 s` above), held only
until the start is fitted; after it the state is §6.4's.

### 6.3 `s = 168`

Specs `s168_airline` `(0,1,1)(0,1,1,168)` and `s168_full`
`(1,1,1)(1,1,1,168)`, the same truths. 10,000 rows are 59 seasons.

**Cost.** One `SARIMAX.loglike` takes about 2 s at 170 states (against 9 ms
at `s = 7`): statsmodels' filter runs its `O(r³)` update on every row until
the state covariance converges to its tolerance, which at a seasonal MA root
of 0.6 takes about `ln(1e-19)/ln(0.36) ≈ 43` seasons, most of the series. A
fit with its OPG standard errors took 144 s on seed 485000 (airline, 6
iterations). Started from the online estimate instead of statsmodels' default
start it took 126 s (5 iterations) and reached the same maximum
(log-likelihood within 1.5e-7, parameters within 6.2e-6): the start does not
change the oracle, nor much the time, so the grid uses the default start. The
fifty-seed grid at `s = 168` is about 100 minutes of four workers; it runs in
the background, resumable (§2), and its counts are in §6.5 as far as they
had run at this commit.

**The plain start fails at `s = 168`.** On seed 485000 (airline) its
estimate at row 9,999 is `(theta, Theta, sigma2) = (-0.368, -0.565, 1.063)`
against `SARIMAX`'s `(-0.395, -0.599, 1.006)`: gaps of +2.9, +4.1 and +3.9
SE. Its path shows why: `Theta` is −0.39, −0.52, −0.55, −0.56, −0.57 at rows

with gain `1/t`, as study 485's ETS at `alpha` 0.6 approached its fit. A
longer warm phase made it worse (`Theta` −0.38 at row 10,000 with a warm phase
of `10 s`). With the batch start (504 rows, fitted in 5 iterations, 0.5 s) the
same seed ends at `(-0.3925, -0.5959, 1.015)`: gaps of +0.23, +0.38 and +0.65
SE.

### 6.4 The state in bytes against `s`

The estimator's state is its rings (each as long as the largest lag a
recursion reads from it), `beta`, the distinct entries of `R`, and six
scalars (`s2`, `W`, the row count and the three warm sums). The table is
computed by `state_table()` and checked against the floats the implementation
allocates for every row. Beside it: the batch start's buffer (held only until
the start is fitted), and what the exact Kalman filter of the scan's
fixed-parameter route would hold, its state and covariance
`8 (r + r(r+1)/2)` with `r = max(p + sP, q + sQ + 1)`.

Ring lengths, with `L_e = q + sQ`:

| ring | length | what reads it |
|---|---|---|
| `y` | `d + sD` | the differencing |
| `w` | `p + sP` | the AR lags (and the lag-1 and lag-`s` warm sums) |
| `e` | `L_e` | the MA lags, and `r` and `z`'s numerators |
| `u` | `max(p, L_e)` if `p > 0` | `psi_phi`, and its own `1/C(B)` recursion |
| `v` | `max(sP, L_e)` if `P > 0` | `psi_Phi` |
| `r` | `max(q, L_e)` if `q > 0` | `psi_theta` |
| `z` | `max(sQ, L_e)` if `Q > 0` | `psi_Theta` |

So with every polynomial of order at most one the state is linear in `s`:

| form | state in bytes | `s = 7` | `s = 24` | `s = 52` | `s = 168` | `s = 336` | `s = 1440` | `s = 8760` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `(0,1,1)(0,1,1,s)` airline | `32 s + 120` | 344 | 888 | 1,784 | 5,496 | 10,872 | 46,200 | 280,440 |
| `(1,1,1)(1,1,1,s)` | `56 s + 216` | 608 | 1,560 | 3,128 | 9,624 | 19,032 | 80,856 | 490,776 |
| `(1,0,1)(1,0,1,s)` | `48 s + 208` | 544 | 1,360 | 2,704 | 8,272 | 16,336 | 69,328 | 420,688 |
| `(1,1,1)(1,1,0,s)` | `24 s + 160` | 328 | 736 | 1,408 | 4,192 | 8,224 | 34,720 | 210,400 |
| batch start buffer (until fitted) | `8 max(500, 3s)` | 4,000 | 4,000 | 4,000 | 4,032 | 8,064 | 34,560 | 210,240 |
| Kalman state and covariance, `(1,1,1)(1,1,1,s)` | `≈ 4 s²` | 432 | 3,016 | 12,312 | 117,640 | 461,032 | 8,334,760 | 307,195,720 |

(Per target and group. The full table, four forms at eleven seasons from 4 to
8,760, prints with `part2`.) The prediction-error estimator's state grows as
`8 (number of rings) s`: four rings of `s + 1` for the airline model, seven
for the full one. At `s = 168` it is 5.4 KB and 9.4 KB, against 115 KB for the
Kalman filter's covariance, which grows as `s²` (8.3 MB at `s = 1,440`,
307 MB at `s = 8,760`); the scan's proposed cap near `r = 256` is not needed
by this estimator. Its `psi` rings could be shared further (each of `r` and
`z` is `e` filtered by `1/C(B)` with a different numerator, so one of them
could be derived), which this study did not try.

The bytes bound the state by the spec's lags, never by the stream (the
bounded-state rule). The cost per row is linear in the number of non-zero
lags, not in `s`: 0.24 to 0.32 s per 10,000 rows in Python at every `s`
measured (`s = 7`, 24, 168).
