seasonal ETS of study 488 with the admissible region held in the batch start
and `beta` as a share of `alpha`, on the 18 sets of 487/488. Parts 2 to 4 (the

## 1. Questions, criterion, environment

1. With the admissible region of the additive-season ETS enforced in the batch
   start, `beta` reparametrised as a share of `alpha`, and (part 2) a Huber
   score, which of the 18 sets of study 488 now pass, with headroom, against
   statsmodels' `ETSModel`? No grid form: the state stays bounded by `m`
   (study 488 §9 costed and the coordinator rejected the grid).
2. (Parts 3 and 4) Asymmetric DCC on study 485's DCC engine against a batch
   ADCC written from the definition with scipy computing the likelihood.

**The criterion** (study 485 and 488, kept): on each of fifty seeds `485000`
to `485049`, at 10,000 rows, every parameter of the online estimate at the
last row within 2 of the oracle's standard errors of the oracle's batch
estimate on all rows; a set passes at 45 of 50 seeds. "With headroom" is
read here as a count at least two seeds above 45 (47 or more) and no seed over
3 SE.

**Environment**: a venv `.venv-493` in the repository root, excluded through
`.git/info/exclude` (`uv venv .venv-493`; `uv pip install numpy scipy
statsmodels`): numpy 2.5.5, scipy 1.18.1, statsmodels 0.15.0, Linux x86-64,
4 cores, one BLAS thread per worker (`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`, set before numpy
loads: set afterwards it does nothing, and four workers ran at a load of 12
until it was moved). Results: `.cache/study_493_488/` (gitignored), one JSON
line per case, so a rerun resumes. The baseline is study 488's cached cases
(`.cache/study_487_488/ets.jsonl`, `ets_mul.jsonl`), the same 18 sets, the same
seeds, the same oracle fits (`ETSModel`, the same settings: study 488 §7.1).

## 2. The state caps (the user's new rules, applied to every figure here)

Every model's data-dependent state needs a stated cap: default **256 KB per
group**, a warning at half (**128 KB**), a refusal at the cap; and a
bank-wide total cap that can be set to infinity. Each model in this record
states its bytes as a function of its shape (`k`, `m`, window, groups), the
check against 256 KB at the shapes run and at the largest `k` or `m`
considered, and the warn point. A bank total is `sum over groups` of the
per-group bytes; with the total cap at infinity only the per-group cap binds.
Part 2 gives the ETS table and part 4 the ADCC table.

## 3. The design (ETS)

**The model and the estimator** are study 488 §7.1's, unchanged: ETS(A,A,A)
and ETS(A,Ad,A) (and the multiplicative-season forms, which divide by the
updated level as `ETSModel` does), `ETSModel`'s parameters `theta = (alpha,
beta, gamma[, phi])`, one Gauss-Newton step per row from carried
sensitivities, gain `1/W`, projection onto `ETSModel`'s box, a batch start of
`max(500, 3m)` rows fitted by `batch_core.maximize` (study 485's core-held
projected Newton), the buffer replayed at the fit, steps from there. Three
changes, each switchable (`Variant` in the script):

**(a) The admissible region** (Hyndman, Koehler, Ord and Snyder 2008, §10.6:
the discount matrix `D = F - g w'` of the error-correction form has every
eigenvalue inside the unit circle but the one exact unit root, the
unobservable direction). The constraint is

```text
r(theta) = max |eigenvalue of D(theta) after the unit root| - 1  <  0
```

held at every point the batch optimizer tries. `batch_core.maximize` only
knows a box projection, so `maximize_region` in the script is the same
algorithm (projected Newton, curvature from central differences of the analytic
gradient, Armijo backtracking, the same stopping rule) with a projection
`Proj` of a candidate `x` from the iterate `theta` that is the box and then
the region:

1. the box (`project_prime`);
2. if `r(x) >= 0`: a step to the linearised boundary in the metric of the
   curvature `M`: `x <- x - lam M^-1 grad r`, `lam = max(0, (r(theta) +
   grad r . (x - theta)) / (grad r' M^-1 grad r))`, `grad r` by forward
   differences of the radius (`p` evaluations, once per iteration and only when
   needed): it slides along the boundary instead of stopping on it;
3. if still `r(x) >= 0`: 8 bisections of the segment `theta -> x`, `theta`
   being admissible.

The start of the optimizer is the best admissible point of the grid (study
488's 12 or 24 points, and two more deep inside the region, `alpha` 0.01 and
0.05, `b` 0.01 and 0.05, `gamma/(1 - alpha)` 0.001, where a long season's
region still has room). The radius is the largest modulus of the eigenvalues
of `D` (`np.linalg.eigvals` of a `(m + 2)`-square matrix), `O(m^3)` per
evaluation. For the multiplicative season the additive `D` at the same
parameters is used (the linearisation about unit seasonal factors); a check
(`mul_region_check`) finds the multiplicative filter's monodromy over one
season has radius equal to `radius ** m` of the additive `D` to 3e-9 (m = 24:
0.97964724 against 0.97964724; m = 7: 0.97726883 against 0.97726883). Only
the batch start is constrained: the online steps are not, as the request
says, and the table below reports where the online parameters end.

**(b) The reparametrisation**: `beta = alpha b`, `b` in `[1e-4, 1 - 1e-4]`,
the box `ETSModel` uses (its `beta/alpha` in `[1e-4, 1 - 1e-4]`), with
`gamma/(1 - alpha)` likewise. The batch fit and the online step both run in
`theta' = (alpha, b, gamma[, phi])`: the carried gradient and information are
mapped through `J = d theta / d theta'` (the identity but for the row
`(b, alpha)` of `beta`): `g' = J' g`, `R' = J' R J`, `theta' <- Proj(theta' +
R'^-1 g' / W)`. The stored state is unchanged (the same floats, `theta` kept;
`b = beta/alpha` is derived).

**(c) The Huber score** (part 2).

**Checks** (`checks()`, 3,000 rows): the radius of `D` against an independent
route, the moving-average polynomial of the innovations form (undamped),
`(1 - B)^2 (1 - B^m) y = Theta(B) e` with `Theta = (1 - B)^2 (1 - B^m) +
B (alpha + beta - alpha B)(1 - B^m) + gamma B^m (1 - B)^2`, whose roots'
reciprocals are `D`'s eigenvalues: 9 points at `m` = 7, 24, 168 agree to
within 5.8e-15 absolute in `r` (the points' `r` run from -1.8e-2 to +2.3e-4); the carried gradient in `theta'` against a central difference
of the objective: 7.4e-9 (ETS(A,Ad,A), m = 24) and 1.1e-7 (ETS(A,A,A), m = 7)
relative (squared error), 6.0e-9 and 1.2e-7 (Huber); out of sample (row 1,500
changed by 100): no prediction up to it moved (0.0); the allocated floats equal
the state count (148, both); the constrained fit ends inside the region.

**Which sets pass.** Counting the criterion as written (45 or more of 50),
study 488's batch start passes **6** of the 18: (A,A,A) and (A,Ad,A) at `m = 7`
on truth A (50, 50), (A,Ad,A) at `m = 168` on truth A (47), (A,A,A) at `m = 24`

above is the count.) `rp` passes **11** and `rp_adm` **10**: the six, less
(A,Ad,A) at `m = 168` under `rp_adm`, plus the truth-B and damped sets at
`m <= 24` that reached 45 to 47 under the reparametrisation ((A,A,A) and
(A,Ad,A) at `m = 7`, (A,Ad,A) at `m = 24`, (A,Ad,A) and (A,Ad,M) at `m = 7`,
(A,Ad,M) at `m = 24`). Those five are at the floor or two seeds above it and
each has a seed 30 to 85 SE off (the ridge, below).

**With headroom** (47 or more of 50 and no seed over 3 SE on any parameter):
`rp_adm` **4**: (A,A,A) and (A,Ad,A) at `m = 7` on truth A (worst 1.31 and
1.28 SE), (A,A,M) at `m = 7` and 24 on truth C (2.49 and 2.15 SE). `rp` has
those four and (A,Ad,A) at `m = 168` on truth A (47, worst 2.44 SE), which the
region removes (5 of 50). Study 488's batch start has the same five. **So no
set newly passes with headroom from the region or the reparametrisation, and
the region loses one.**

**What the reparametrisation did** (`rp` against 488): the damped sets with
small `beta` gain 1 to 4 seeds (43 to 46, 33 to 37, 44 to 45, 34 to 36) and
the worst `beta` gap on the damped truth-B sets falls on some (56.6 to 51.1,
63.9 to 34.1, 67.3 to 27.9, 63 to 30.7) but not all (81.3 to 85.1 at `m = 24`
multiplicative). It does not remove the `(alpha, beta, phi)` ridge: the same
seeds (485020 above all) still end with `phi` at 0.8 and `beta` about 0.16
against 0.005 to 0.02, 20 to 85 SE off. Counted over the six damped sets, 488
passes 44, 43, 33, 44, 44, 34 and `rp` 47, 46, 37, 45, 45, 36 of 50: 13 more
seeds in 300. Undamped truth B at `m <= 24` moves by at most 1 (44 to 45).

**What the region did** (`rp_adm` against `rp`):

1. At `m = 7` and at `m = 24` on every truth-B and truth-C set, nothing: the
   oracle is inside the region on all 50 seeds, so is the unconstrained fit,
   and the counts are identical to the seed (the batch fit is the same point;
   `rp_adm` only adds radius evaluations, 29 to 138 per fit at the median).
2. At `m = 24` on truth A (outside the region by construction) the region
   makes the batch fit lie on the boundary (`r = -2e-15` against `rp`'s
   `+1.5e-3`) and halves or better the damage: (A,A,A) online MSE against the
   batch parameters' 1.68 to 1.14 (median ratio) and 4.1e8 to 4.9 (max): the
   unconstrained fit's filter diverges on some seeds, and under the region none
   exceeds 4.9. The count stays 0 and 2 of 50: the oracle sits outside the region
   on 47 and 49 seeds, so the online estimate of 0 to 2 SE of an
   outside-the-region oracle is out of reach by construction.
3. **At `m = 168` it makes the counts fall**: (A,A,A) truth A 26 to 0,
   (A,Ad,A) A 47 to 5, (A,A,A) truth B 37 to 0, (A,Ad,A) B 37 to 36. The
   oracle lies outside the region on 50 of 50 seeds on the first three sets
   (24 of 50 on damped truth B, 10 of 50 on multiplicative C), so the
   constrained fit is held to the boundary while `ETSModel`'s point is beyond
   it; the gaps then read 56 to 8.7e3 SE on `sigma2` and 6 SE on `beta` (the
   cause on `sigma2` was not isolated here). On the seeds where the oracle is
   inside (26 of 50 on (A,Ad,A) truth B, 40 of 50 on (A,A,M) C) the count is
   within one seed of the unconstrained (18 against 19, 25 against 25).

**The online parameters leave the region anyway.** Only the batch start is
constrained, and the Gauss-Newton steps are not: at row 10,000 the
online parameters are outside the region on 7 of 50 seeds ((A,A,A) m = 24
truth A), 19 of 50 ((A,Ad,A) m = 24 A), 48, 50 ((A,A,A) and (A,Ad,A) m = 168
A), 50 ((A,A,A) m = 168 B), 23 ((A,Ad,A) m = 168 B), 29 ((A,A,M) m = 168 C)
and 22 ((A,Ad,M) m = 168 B), and on none at `m = 7`. At `m = 168` the region is a
sliver on the unit circle (within 3.5e-5 of it for every point scanned in
study 488 §7.2), and the one-row step moves the parameters off it. The batch
start's constraint does not keep the stream inside; that needs the region in
`Proj` at every step (a `O(m^3)` eigenvalue problem per row, or a cheaper
sufficient condition, which this study did not try) and so was not claimed.

**Cost** (medians per seed): where the region binds the batch fit takes more
objective evaluations (171 against 62 at (A,A,A) m = 24 A; 114 against 66 at m
= 168 B) and where it does not about the same; the radius itself is 29 to 139
evaluations of a `(m + 2)`-square eigenvalue problem per fit. The whole online
Python run, batch fit included, goes from 1.3 to 2.7 s at (A,A,A) m = 24 A, 1.4
to 3.4 s at m = 168 B and 5.4 to 17.9 s on (A,A,M) m = 168 C; every case under
30 s.

**Reading.** On the admissible region and the reparametrisation alone,
study 488's conclusion stands: the recursive estimator has headroom on four
sets (additive at `m = 7` on truth A, multiplicative undamped at `m <= 24`), the region
cannot rescue the sets whose oracle is itself outside it (`ETSModel` does not
enforce its own region and lands outside on 47 to 50 of 50 seeds at `m >= 24`
on truth A and at `m = 168` on A and B), and the small-`beta` ridge needs
something other than the reparametrisation. Whether "the same as `ETSModel`"
is the right target at `m >= 24` on truth A is a question for the coordinator:
the oracle there is a non-forecastable fit. Part 2 tests the Huber score on
the same 18 sets.
