## 2. The state caps (the user's new rules, applied to every figure here)

Every model's data-dependent state needs a stated cap: default **256 KB per
group**, a warning at half (**128 KB**), a refusal at the cap; and a
bank-wide total cap that can be set to infinity. Each model in this record
states its bytes as a function of its shape (`k`, `m`, window, groups), the
check against 256 KB at the shapes run and at the largest `k` or `m`
considered, and the warn point. A bank total is `sum over groups` of the
per-group bytes; with the total cap at infinity only the per-group cap binds.
§6 gives the ETS table and part 4 the ADCC table.

## 3. The design (ETS)

**The model and the estimator** are study 488 §7.1's, unchanged: ETS(A,A,A)
and ETS(A,Ad,A) (and the multiplicative-season forms, which divide by the
updated level as `ETSModel` does), `ETSModel`'s parameters `theta = (alpha,
beta, gamma[, phi])`, one Gauss-Newton step per row from carried
sensitivities, gain `1/W`, projection onto `ETSModel`'s box, a batch start of
`max(500, 3m)` rows fitted by `batch_core.maximize` (study 485's core-held
projected Newton), the buffer replayed at the fit, steps from there. Three
changes, each switchable (`Variant` in the script):

**(a) The admissible region** (Hyndman, Koehler, Ord and Snyder 2008, §10.6:
the discount matrix `D = F - g w'` of the error-correction form has every
eigenvalue inside the unit circle but the one exact unit root, the
unobservable direction). The constraint is

```text
r(theta) = max |eigenvalue of D(theta) after the unit root| - 1  <  0
```

held at every point the batch optimizer tries. `batch_core.maximize` only
knows a box projection, so `maximize_region` in the script is the same
algorithm (projected Newton, curvature from central differences of the analytic
gradient, Armijo backtracking, the same stopping rule) with a projection
`Proj` of a candidate `x` from the iterate `theta` that is the box and then
the region:

1. the box (`project_prime`);
2. if `r(x) >= 0`: a step to the linearised boundary in the metric of the
   curvature `M`: `x <- x - lam M^-1 grad r`, `lam = max(0, (r(theta) +
   grad r . (x - theta)) / (grad r' M^-1 grad r))`, `grad r` by forward
   differences of the radius (`p` evaluations, once per iteration and only when
   needed): it slides along the boundary instead of stopping on it;
3. if still `r(x) >= 0`: 8 bisections of the segment `theta -> x`, `theta`
   being admissible.

The start of the optimizer is the best admissible point of the grid (study
488's 12 or 24 points, and two more deep inside the region, `alpha` 0.01 and
0.05, `b` 0.01 and 0.05, `gamma/(1 - alpha)` 0.001, where a long season's
region still has room). The radius is the largest modulus of the eigenvalues
of `D` (parts 1 and 2 used `np.linalg.eigvals` of a `(m + 2)`-square
matrix; §8.1 replaces it with a polynomial route of `O(m)` memory), `O(m^3)` per
evaluation. For the multiplicative season the additive `D` at the same
parameters is used (the linearisation about unit seasonal factors); a check
(`mul_region_check`) finds the multiplicative filter's monodromy over one
season has radius equal to `radius ** m` of the additive `D` to 3e-9 (m = 24:
0.97964724 against 0.97964724; m = 7: 0.97726883 against 0.97726883). Only
the batch start is constrained: the online steps are not, as the request
says, and the table below reports where the online parameters end.

**(b) The reparametrisation**: `beta = alpha b`, `b` in `[1e-4, 1 - 1e-4]`,
the box `ETSModel` uses (its `beta/alpha` in `[1e-4, 1 - 1e-4]`), with
`gamma/(1 - alpha)` likewise. The batch fit and the online step both run in
`theta' = (alpha, b, gamma[, phi])`: the carried gradient and information are
mapped through `J = d theta / d theta'` (the identity but for the row
`(b, alpha)` of `beta`): `g' = J' g`, `R' = J' R J`, `theta' <- Proj(theta' +
R'^-1 g' / W)`. The stored state is unchanged (the same floats, `theta` kept;
`b = beta/alpha` is derived).

**(c) The Huber score** (part 2). `rho(e) = e^2/2` for `|e| <= c` and
`c (|e| - c/2)` beyond, with the fixed constant `c = 1.345` (the 95% efficiency
constant of a unit-variance normal; the series' innovations have unit
variance and the constant is not scaled by an estimate, so it adds no state).
The batch fit maximizes `-sum rho(e_t)` from the known initial states with the
gradient `sum rho'(e_t) psi_t` (`rho'` the clipped residual) and the
Gauss-Newton matrix `sum rho''(e_t) psi_t psi_t'` (`rho'' = 1` inside `c`, 0
outside); the online step uses the clipped residual in the gradient,
`g' = J' (psi rho'(e))`, and keeps `R` the running mean of `psi psi'` (not
weighted by `rho''`) and `s2` the running mean of `e^2` unclipped, so `sigma2`
still estimates the variance `ETSModel` reports. The oracle is `ETSModel`'s
Gaussian fit: on Gaussian data the Huber estimate is consistent for the same
parameters and differs by its efficiency loss, about
`sqrt(1/0.95 - 1) = 0.23` SE at the constant used.

**Checks** (`checks()`, 3,000 rows): the radius of `D` against an independent
route, the moving-average polynomial of the innovations form (undamped),
`(1 - B)^2 (1 - B^m) y = Theta(B) e` with `Theta = (1 - B)^2 (1 - B^m) +
B (alpha + beta - alpha B)(1 - B^m) + gamma B^m (1 - B)^2`, whose roots'
reciprocals are `D`'s eigenvalues: 9 points at `m` = 7, 24, 168 agree to
within 5.8e-15 absolute in `r` (the points' `r` run from -1.8e-2 to +2.3e-4); the carried gradient in `theta'` against a central difference
of the objective: 7.4e-9 (ETS(A,Ad,A), m = 24) and 1.1e-7 (ETS(A,A,A), m = 7)
relative (squared error), 6.0e-9 and 1.2e-7 (Huber); out of sample (row 1,500
changed by 100): no prediction up to it moved (0.0); the allocated floats equal
the state count (148, both); the constrained fit ends inside the region.

## 6. The state against the caps (ETS)

The user's rule (§2): 256 KB (262,144 B) per group by default, a warning at
128 KB (131,072 B), a refusal at the cap, a bank total that may be infinite.
None of the three changes of this study adds state: the reparametrisation
stores `theta` (`b = beta/alpha` is derived), the region is a check, and the
Huber constant is a fixed number. The state is study 488 §7.4's, checked
against the floats the estimator allocates at `m` = 7, 24, 168 and 1,440, both
forms (`allocation_check`: equal in all eight):

```text
state(k, m) = 8 ((k + 1)(m + 2) + k + k(k+1)/2 + 4) bytes
   ETS(A,A,A)   k = 3:  32 m + 168        ETS(A,Ad,A)  k = 4:  40 m + 224
buffer(m)   = 8 max(500, 3 m) bytes, held until the batch start is fitted
peak(m)     = state(m) + buffer(m)         (both are allocated at once)
```

## 7. Part 3: asymmetric DCC at two series

### 7.1 The model

Cappiello, Engle and Sheppard (2006), on standardized residuals `z_t = r_t /
sigma_t` (the margins' variances), `n_t = z_t * (z_t < 0)` elementwise:

```text
Q_t = Qbar - a Qbar - b Qbar - g N + a z_{t-1} z_{t-1}' + g n_{t-1} n_{t-1}' + b Q_{t-1}
Qbar = mean z z',   N = mean n n',   R_t = diag(Q_t)^(-1/2) Q_t diag(Q_t)^(-1/2)
```

with `g = 0` it is DCC(1,1) (a check holds `r_path` of the ADCC code at
`g = 0` equal to study 485's `dcc.r_path_k` to 0.0). **The admissible set** is
`a, b, g >= 0` and `a + b + delta g < 1` with `delta` the largest eigenvalue
of `Qbar^(-1/2) N Qbar^(-1/2)`: the intercept `C = (1 - a - b) Qbar - g N` is
`Qbar^(1/2) ((1 - a - b) I - g Qbar^(-1/2) N Qbar^(-1/2)) Qbar^(1/2)`, positive
semi-definite exactly when `1 - a - b >= g delta`, and then every `Q_t` is
(the sum of a PSD intercept, PSD `zz'` and `nn'` terms and a positive multiple
of the previous `Q`). That is the condition's role here, and it is derived. It
is not claimed to be the process's stationarity condition (CES 2006 give it
for that, under their assumptions; not re-derived here, and not needed for the
comparison). The estimators project onto it: clip at 0 and, if the sum is over
`1 - 1e-4`, the Lagrange form `theta_i = max(0, x_i - lam c_i)`, `c = (1, 1,
delta)`, `lam` by bisection (the exact Euclidean projection).

### 7.2 The step that was not settled: which `N` the online side uses

The request defines `N` "as the sample mean of `n n'` over the start window".
Study 485's engine carries `Qbar` as the running mean of `z z'` over every row
read (the scan's online targeting). **The online ADCC here carries `N` the
same way, as the running mean of `n n'`**, and the oracle takes the full-sample
means of both, so that online and batch estimate the same `(a, b, g)`: a `N`
frozen at the 500-row start window would differ from the full-sample `N` by its
own sampling error (about `1/sqrt(500)` relative), which the batch fit does
not share and which would show up as a bias in `g`, not as a property of the
online step. That is a reading of the request, not a derivation: a start-window
`N` was **not run**; if it is wanted, the comparison needs an oracle that
fixes `N` the same way. Whichever is chosen, the state is the same (one
symmetric matrix).

### 7.3 The estimator (the A12 engine, extended by one parameter)

`OnlineAdccT` (in `scripts/study_493_488.py`) subclasses study 485's
`OnlineDccT`. Unchanged: the margins (targeted online GARCH(1,1), batch start of
500 rows by the core's optimizer, Huber score 2), the shared start (each

### 7.4 The oracle

Written from the definition: arch's Gaussian GARCH(1,1) per margin (`z` from its
in-sample volatility), `Qbar` and `N` the full-sample means, `Q_0 = Qbar`, the
`Q` recursion by `scipy.signal.lfilter`, and `(a, b, g)` (Gaussian oracle) or
`(a, b, g, nu)` (t oracle) maximizing the second-stage log-likelihood by
`scipy.optimize.minimize(method="SLSQP")` from `(0.03, 0.85, 0.02[, 8])`,
`a, b, g >= 0`, `a + b + delta g <= 1 - 1e-4`, `nu` in `[2.1, 200]`, `ftol`
1e-12; sandwich standard errors `H^-1 J'J H^-1` by statsmodels' numerical
differentiation (the second stage only, as study 485 §14, accepted for DCC).
Both oracles reported success on all 200 fits (at most 25 iterations for the
Gaussian one and 63 for the t one).

**Where scipy computes the likelihood.** The per-row likelihood is written by
hand and vectorized (the correlation-stage Gaussian row `-1/2 (ln|R| + z'R^-1 z
- z'z)`, the multivariate-t row of study 485). `scipy.stats` computes it
independently, row by row: `multivariate_normal.logpdf(z_t; R_t)` less the
independent `logpdf(z_t; I)`, and `multivariate_t.logpdf(z_t; shape = R_t (nu -
2)/nu, df = nu)`, at each oracle's optimum, **on every case**. Agreement: at
most 1.8e-13 per row (Gaussian oracle's likelihood at most 5.7e-14, t's at
most 1.8e-13) and 1.7e-9 in the sum over 10,000 rows, over all 200 comparisons. It is not inside the
optimizer's loop: one full `scipy.stats` evaluation of 10,000 rows takes 0.65
to 0.7 s and SLSQP uses about 100 to 300 evaluations (with its finite
differences), minutes per fit, 100 fits per part. So scipy's likelihood
certifies the hand-written one at the point the optimizer reached; it does not
choose that point.

**Checks** (`adcc_checks`, 3,000 rows): `g = 0` equals the DCC path (0.0); the
`lfilter` path equals a direct loop of the definition as written above (7.8e-16);
the start objective's analytic gradient equals a central difference (7.9e-9
relative); the online row score equals a central difference of the row's t
log-density in `(a, b, g)` (8.0e-11 relative); out of sample (row 150 shifted by
5): no emitted correlation up to it moved (0.0); the hand likelihood equals
`scipy.stats`'s on 1,000 rows (3.6e-15 both).

## 8. Part 4: the region check on `O(m)` memory, ADCC at three series, the open items

### 8.1 The region check without the `(m + 2)`-square matrix

Working memory counts toward the bounded-memory rule, transient or not, so the
batch start's region check was rebuilt (`radius_poly`, `admissible`). With
damping `phi` (`phi = 1` undamped) the innovations form is

```text
(1 - B)(1 - phi B)(1 - B^m) y_t = Theta(B) e_t
Theta = S(B)(1 - B^m) + gamma B^m (1 - B)(1 - phi B),
S = 1 + (alpha + phi beta - 1 - phi) B + phi (1 - alpha) B^2
```

(from `y/e = B [alpha + phi beta B/(1 - phi B)]/(1 - B) + phi beta B/(1 - phi B)
+ gamma B^m/(1 - B^m) + 1`; at `phi = 1` it is part 1's polynomial). The
eigenvalues of `D` are the reciprocals of `Theta`'s `m + 2` roots; `Theta(1) = 0`
is the unit root, and `Theta/(1 - B)` (the running sum of the coefficients) has
the other `m + 1`. "Inside the region" is "every root of that quotient
outside the unit circle", decided by the **Schur-Cohn step-down** in `O(m^2)`
time and `O(m)` memory (no companion matrix; each step rescaled by its largest
entry), and the radius is the smallest scale `s` for which the roots scaled by
`s` clear the circle, by bisection to 1e-14 (inside `1 +- min(0.25, 300/m)`, so
no scaled coefficient overflows; a radius outside that band is returned as its
edge). The optimizer's boundary tests (every candidate of the line search, the
start grid) are one Schur-Cohn test each; the value is bisected only for `r` at
the iterate and the gradient's differences.

**Against the matrix route** (`radius_route_check`): 120 random points per
`(m, damped)`, `m` = 7, 24, 168, both forms, alpha, beta/alpha and
gamma/(1 - alpha) log-uniform over the box (many points within 1e-6 of the
edge: 3, 9, 32, 52 at `m` = 24 and 168): largest `|r|` difference 2.9e-13 and

### 8.4 (b) The stationarity condition: not derived

What is derived (§7.1): `a + b + delta g <= 1` makes the intercept `C = (1 - a - b)
Qbar - g N` positive semi-definite for the sample `Qbar` and `N`, so every `Q_t`
is. What is **not derived, and not claimed**: that it is the condition for the
process's covariance stationarity, as CES (2006) state it. The obstacle: the
mean recursion `E[Q_t] = (1 - a - b) Qbar - g N + a E[z z'] + g E[n n'] + b
E[Q_{t-1}]` needs `E[z z']` and `E[n n']`, but `E[z z' | F] = R_t` (the
correlation, with `Q`'s scale normalized away) and `E[n n' | F]` is a nonlinear
function of `R_t` (orthant probabilities for Gaussian noise, others for t). A
bound on how fast `E[n n']` moves with `Q` is what turns the recursion's
stability into `a + b + delta g < 1`; `delta`, a ratio at the fixed point, is
not that bound as far as this study could settle. **Flag for a derivation.**
It is used here only as the estimators' constraint set. Empirically: every
oracle fit (400 across 2 and 3 series) is inside it and none sits on its
boundary, the 100 simulated series per setting stay finite and positive
definite, and the pilot's `E[n n']` iteration converged (§7.3).

## 9. Request A18: the covariance stationarity of ADCC's `Q`

*Part 1 (this section's §9.1 to §9.8): the derivation, with each step, and the
paper's statement checked. Parts 2 and 3 (simulation at 2 and 3 series near the
boundary, and the bound the build should enforce) follow in later commits.
The runnable study is `scripts/study_493_stationarity.py` (`part1` computes
every number quoted here; cached in `.cache/study_493_488/stationarity_part1.json`).*

### 9.1 The process and the assumptions

```text
Q_{t+1} = C + a z_t z_t' + g n_t n_t' + b Q_t,     C = (1 - a - b) Qbar - g N
R_t = D_t^(-1/2) Q_t D_t^(-1/2),  D_t = diag(Q_t),   z_t = S_t eps_t,  S_t S_t' = R_t,   n_t = min(z_t, 0)
```

(the §7.1 recursion with the time index moved by one). Assumed: `a, b, g >= 0`;
`Qbar` positive definite with unit diagonal (the targeting `Qbar = E z z'` has
it, since each `z_i` has unit variance); `N` a fixed symmetric matrix ("N
treated as known"; the running `N` of the engine is not covered by the
derivation, only by part 2's simulation); `Q_0` positive definite; `eps_t` iid,
**spherical** (the law of `U eps` equals that of `eps` for every orthogonal
`U`), `E eps eps' = I`, with a density positive at 0: the standard normal, or
the Student-t(nu) scaled to unit variance, `eps = sqrt((nu - 2)/chi2_nu) x`.
`kappa = E eps_1^4`: 3 for the normal, `3 (nu - 2)/(nu - 4)` for the t, **9 at
`nu = 5`** (both from `scipy.stats`' kurtosis). `F_t` is the history up to
`Q_t` (so `Q_t` and `R_t` are `F_t`-measurable and `eps_t` is independent of
`F_t`). For a spherical `eps` the law of `S eps` depends on `S` only through
`S S' = R`, so the choice of square root (Cholesky, symmetric) does not matter.

### 9.2 Step 1: the forcing sees `Q` only through `R`, which is scale-free and bounded

(a) `E[z_t z_t' | F_t] = S_t E[eps eps'] S_t' = R_t`. `R_t` is a correlation
matrix: unit diagonal, entries in `[-1, 1]`, largest eigenvalue at most `k`.

(b) For each `i`, `z_{i,t} = s_i' eps_t` with `s_i` the `i`-th row of `S_t`
and `|s_i|^2 = (R_t)_ii = 1`. For a spherical `eps` and any unit vector `s`,
`s' eps` has the law of `eps_1`. So **each `z_{i,t}` is independent of `F_t`,
and the sequence `z_{i,0}, z_{i,1}, ...` is iid with the law of `eps_1`**, for
each `i` on its own (the vector `z_t` is not independent of `F_t`: its
correlation is `R_t`).

(c) Hence `E[n_i^2 | F_t] = E[eps_1^2 1(eps_1 < 0)] = 1/2` by symmetry, and
`E[n_i n_j | F_t] = h(rho_ij,t)`, a function of the pair's correlation alone
(the pair `(z_i, z_j)` is the bivariate marginal of a spherical law with
correlation `rho_ij,t`), with `|h| <= 1/2` (Cauchy-Schwarz). For the normal,
`h(rho) = (sqrt(1 - rho^2) + rho (pi - arccos rho)) / (2 pi)`: 0.3044989 at
`rho = 0.5`; scipy's quadrature of the bivariate density agrees to 2.2e-16 at
`rho` = -0.5, 0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.9.

(d) Fourth moments. `E[|z_t|^4 | F_t] = E[(eps' R_t eps)^2] <= lambda_max(R_t)^2
E|eps|^4 <= k^2 E|eps|^4`, and for a spherical law `E|eps|^4 = k kappa + k (k -
1) kappa / 3 = k (k + 2) kappa / 3` (because `E eps_1^4 = 3 E eps_1^2 eps_2^2`
for spherical laws). Finite exactly when `kappa` is: always for the normal, for
the t only when `nu > 4`. `|n_t| <= |z_t|`, so `n n'` is bounded the same way.

Write `U_t = a z_t z_t' + g n_t n_t'`. Steps (a) to (d) say: **the conditional
law of `U_t` given `F_t` depends on `Q_t` only through `R_t`, and its mean and
second moment are bounded uniformly over every `Q_t`**:
`|E[U_t | F_t]|_max <= a + g/2`, `E[|U_t|_F^2 | F_t] <= (a + g)^2 k^2 k (k + 2) kappa / 3`.

### 9.3 Step 2: the mean, and the matrix of its recursion

Take expectations of the recursion (`E[U_t] = E[E[U_t | F_t]]`):

```text
E Q_{t+1} = C + a E R_t + g E H_t + b E Q_t,       H_t = E[n_t n_t' | F_t] = [h(rho_ij,t)]
vec m_{t+1} = (b I_{k^2}) vec m_t + vec f_t,          f_t = C + a E R_t + g E H_t
```

`f_t` is an input whose entries are bounded by `|C_ij| + a + g/2` whatever
`m_t` is (step 1). So **the matrix of the mean recursion is `b I_{k^2}`; its
spectral radius is `b`.** `a`, `g`, `Qbar`, `N` and `delta` are not in it:
they set the intercept and the input's size, not the propagation.

Why not `a + b` as in GARCH: in a scalar GARCH `h_{t+1} = w + a e_t^2 + b h_t`
with `e_t^2 = h_t eta_t^2`, so `E[a e_t^2 | F_t] = a h_t` is proportional to the
state and joins the matrix, which becomes `a + b`. In (A)DCC the input is
`z_t`, standardized by the margins, whose conditional covariance is `R_t`, that
is `Q_t` with its scale divided out: `E[z z' | F_t] = R_t`, not `Q_t`. `Q`'s
level never reaches the input. The input does depend on `Q_t` (through `R_t`,
nonlinearly, and through `h` for the `n` part), but it is bounded, so it acts
as a bounded forcing, not as a coefficient.

Solving: `m_t = b^t m_0 + sum_{j < t} b^j f_{t-1-j}`, bounded for all `t`
when `b < 1`, with `|m_t - b^t m_0|_max <= (max |C_ij| + a + g/2) / (1 - b)`. If
a stationary law exists (§9.6), its mean `mu` solves `(1 - b) mu = C + a E R +
g E H`; with `Qbar = E[z z'] = E R` and `N = E[n n'] = E H` (the targets
computed from the process itself), `(1 - b) mu = (1 - a - b) Qbar - g N + a Qbar
+ g N = (1 - b) Qbar`: **the targeting is consistent, `mu = Qbar`**, for every
`(a, b, g)` with `b < 1`.

**The diagonal is exact.** By step 1(b), `q_ii` follows the scalar AR(1)
`q_ii,t+1 = C_ii + u_i,t + b q_ii,t` with `u_i,t = z_i,t^2 (a + g 1(z_i,t < 0))`
**iid** (no dependence on `Q` at all). So `E q_ii = (C_ii + a + g/2)/(1 - b)`
(= 1 when `Qbar_ii = 1` and `N_ii = 1/2`), and, step 3 below, `Var q_ii =
Var u / (1 - b^2)` with `Var u = kappa (a^2 + a g + g^2/2) - (a + g/2)^2`
(from `E[z^4 (a + g 1(z < 0))^2] = kappa a^2 + kappa (2 a g + g^2)/2`), and the
autocorrelation at lag `h` is `b^h`. At the truth `(0.03, 0.93, 0.03)`:
`Var q_ii` = **0.0350** (normal) and **0.1349** (t(5)). These are the
simulation's exact targets for the diagonal (parts 2 and 3).

### 9.4 Step 3: the second moments

The moving-average form `Q_t = b^t Q_0 + sum_{j=0}^{t-1} b^j (C + U_{t-1-j})`
and Minkowski's inequality in `L^2` (Frobenius norm) give

```text
(E |Q_t|_F^2)^(1/2) <= b^t |Q_0|_F + (|C|_F + sup_s (E |U_s|_F^2)^(1/2)) / (1 - b)
                    <= b^t |Q_0|_F + (|C|_F + (a + g) k sqrt(k (k + 2) kappa / 3)) / (1 - b)
```

uniformly in `t`: **bounded when `b < 1` and `kappa < infinity`.** In the
form the request asks for: `S_t = E[vec Q_t vec Q_t']` obeys `S_{t+1} = b^2 S_t
+ b (M_t + M_t') + E[(c + u_t)(c + u_t)']` with `M_t = E[vec Q_t (c + E[u_t |
F_t])']`, `c = vec C`, `u_t = vec U_t`; the last two terms are inputs bounded
by step 1 and `|M_t| <= const * E|Q_t|` (bounded by step 2). The matrix acting on
`S_t` is `b^2 I_{k^4}`, **spectral radius `b^2`**. Again `a`, `g`, `Qbar`, `N`,
`delta` are not in it.

Two consequences that differ from GARCH. (i) There is no separate "fourth
moment boundary" inside the region: `Var q_ii` is finite for every `b < 1`; it
grows as the boundary is approached only because `a` or `g` grows (the input's
variance), not through a vanishing denominator. (ii) **The t's degrees of
freedom enter: for `nu <= 4`, `kappa = infinity` and `Var q_ii = infinity`
however small `a, b, g` are** (the mean is still bounded for `nu > 2`). So `Q`
is not covariance stationary under a t(4) or heavier noise, anywhere in the
region. The online engine fits `nu` in `[2.1, 200]` (§7.3).

### 9.5 Step 4: the process must stay defined, and that is where `delta` enters

`R_t` exists (a correlation matrix with a square root, so `z_t` can be drawn)
only while `Q_t` is positive semi-definite with a positive diagonal. Steps 2
and 3 assumed it.

**Sufficient: `C` PSD.** Then `Q_t = b^t Q_0 + (PSD terms)` is at least
`b^t Q_0`, positive definite (for `b > 0`). §7.1: for `Qbar` PD, `C = Qbar^(1/2)
((1 - a - b) I - g Qbar^(-1/2) N Qbar^(-1/2)) Qbar^(1/2)` is PSD exactly when
`1 - a - b >= g delta`, `delta = lambda_max(Qbar^(-1/2) N Qbar^(-1/2))`.

**Necessary: if `v' C v < 0` for some `v`, `Q_t` leaves the PSD cone in finite
time, with probability one.** Proof (written here; not in the paper). Let
`Q~` be the process with `R_t` replaced, once `Q_t` is not PSD, by the
eigenvalue-clipped and rediagonalized correlation (any rule keeping a unit
diagonal and `lambda_max <= k`); it equals `Q` up to the first exit time `T`,
and step 3's bound holds for it (it used only `lambda_max(R) <= k`), so
`E |v'Q~_t v| <= M_0` for all `t`. Choose `eta > 0` with `(a + g) k eta^2 |v|^2 <=
|v'Cv| / 2`, then for a level `M` choose `J` with `b^J M + (1 - b^J) v'Cv / (2 (1
- b)) < 0`. Let `A_t` = {`|eps_s| < eta` for `s = t, ..., t + J - 1`}: `P(A_t) =
p^J > 0` (the density is positive at 0), independent of `F_t`. On `A_t`,
`|z_s|^2 = eps_s' R~_s eps_s <= k eta^2` and `(v'n_s)^2 <= |z_s|^2 |v|^2`, so

```text
v' Q~_{t+J} v = b^J v'Q~_t v + sum_{j<J} b^j (v'Cv + a (v'z)^2 + g (v'n)^2)
             <= b^J M + (1 - b^J) v'Cv / (2 (1 - b)) < 0     on A_t and {v'Q~_t v <= M}.
```

So `P(T > (r + 1) J | F_{rJ}) <= 1 - p^J 1(v'Q~_{rJ} v <= M)` on `{T > rJ}`, and
with `P(v'Q~_{rJ} v > M) <= M_0/M =: e` (Markov), `P(T > (r + 1) J) <= (1 - p^J) P(T > rJ)
+ p^J e`, whose limit in `r` is at most `e`. `M` is arbitrary, so `P(T =
infinity) <= M_0/M` for every `M`: `P(T = infinity) = 0`.

Therefore, for `a, g >= 0`, `Q_0` PD and noise with a density positive at 0:
**`Q_t` stays PSD for all `t` with probability one exactly when `a + b + delta g
<= 1`.** Outside, the failure is not a moment that grows: it is `Q` leaving the
cone (a diagonal entry or a principal minor going negative), after which the
model is undefined. The rate: the derivation gives finiteness only; how soon it
happens just outside is for the simulation (parts 2 and 3).

### 9.6 Existence of a stationary solution; what is not derived

Strictly inside (`a + b + delta g < 1`, which with `a, g >= 0` forces `b < 1`):
`C_ii = 1 - a - b - g N_ii > 0`, because `N_ii <= delta Qbar_ii = delta` (the
Rayleigh quotient at `e_i`). The set `X = {Q PSD : q_ii >= C_ii/(1 - b) for each
i}` is closed and invariant (`q_ii,t+1 >= C_ii + b q_ii,t`), contains `Qbar`
(`C_ii <= 1 - b`), and on it `Q -> R(Q)` is continuous, so the transition is
Feller. Step 3 bounds the second moments, so the time-averaged laws are tight;
by Krylov-Bogoliubov an invariant law exists, with finite second moments by
Fatou. Started from it, `Q` is strictly and covariance stationary.

**Not derived: uniqueness of the stationary law and convergence to it from an
arbitrary start (ergodicity).** A contraction argument would have to bound how
fast `R(Q)` moves with `Q` (Lipschitz constant about `1 / min q_ii`), and that is
where `a` would re-enter a condition; Fermanian and Malongo (arXiv 1405.6905),
for a scalar DCC, state existence under one condition and uniqueness under a
stronger one that reads `a + b < 1` in their scalar example (read through a
summarizing tool, not line by line, and their model is not this one's targeted
form). The simulation's "the running mean settles to the same value on every
seed" is the evidence for it here, not a proof.

### 9.7 The condition

| question | condition | `delta`? |
|---|---|---|
| the mean recursion's matrix | `b I_{k^2}`, spectral radius `b`: bounded mean iff `b < 1` | no |
| the second-moment recursion's matrix | `b^2 I_{k^4}`: bounded variance iff `b < 1`, given `E eps_1^4 < infinity` (t: `nu > 4`) | no |
| `Q_t` defined (PSD) for all `t`, a.s. | `C = (1 - a - b) Qbar - g N` PSD, i.e. **`a + b + delta g <= 1`**, necessary and sufficient | **yes** |
| a covariance stationary solution exists | `a, g >= 0`, **`a + b + delta g < 1`** (implies `b < 1`), and `E eps_1^4 < infinity` | yes, through positivity |
| it is unique / reached from any start | not derived | - |

So **the expression is `a + b + delta g < 1` with `delta = lambda_max(Qbar^(-1/2)
N Qbar^(-1/2))`, plus `nu > 4` for a t noise**, as the record already used, but
for a different reason than "spectral radius of the moment recursion": that
radius is `b`, and `delta` does not enter it. `delta` enters because outside
`a + b + delta g <= 1` the process ceases to exist (step 4), not because a
moment explodes. In terms of `Qbar` and `N` the condition is a matrix
inequality, `(1 - a - b) Qbar - g N >= 0` (PSD); `delta` is its scalar form.

### 9.8 The paper's statement, checked

Read: the working-paper version (Cappiello, Engle and Sheppard, "Asymmetric
Dynamics in the Correlations of Global Equity and Bond Returns", the IGIER
Bocconi PDF; its file is titled "Draft010.06.2002", modified 2003), in full by `pdftotext`. Its model, eq. (5):
`Q_t = (Qbar - A'Qbar A - B'Qbar B - G'N G) + A' e e' A + B' Q_{t-1} B + G' n n'
G`, `A, B, G` diagonal; the scalar ADCC is `A = [a]`, `B = [b]`, `G = [g]`, so
the paper's scalar parameters enter **squared** (its `a^2, b^2, g^2` are this
record's `a, b, g`). Its only statement on the point, footnote 9, verbatim:
"Q_t will be positive definite with probability one if (Q − A′Q A − B′Q B −
G′Q G) is positive definite." (`G'QG` there is, by eq. (5), a typo for `G'NG`;
that is a reading.) The word "stationary" appears once, for the univariate
margins. There is no `delta` and no stationarity condition for the asymmetric
model in that version.

Not read: the published version (J. Financial Econometrics 4(4), 2006). The
`delta` statement is cited from it (p. 544) by secondary sources, seen only
through a search tool's summaries, which describe it as a restriction
**sufficient for positive definiteness**, `delta` the largest eigenvalue of
`Qbar^(-1/2) N Qbar^(-1/2)`, written in the paper's squared parameters (one
gives `a^2 + b^2 + delta g^2 < 1`). Not verified verbatim.

**Agreement.** The paper's condition, in both forms seen, is a
positive-definiteness condition, and it agrees with step 4's sufficiency
(identical after `a -> a^2`, etc.). Differences: (i) the paper states it as
sufficient ("if"); step 4 shows it is also necessary for the process to stay
defined; (ii) the paper asks the intercept to be PD, PSD suffices for `Q_t`
PD (with `Q_0` PD and `b > 0`); (iii) the paper, as read, gives no covariance
stationarity condition for the asymmetric model, and says nothing of the
fourth moment, which step 3 needs (`nu > 4`). **Correction to §7.1 and §8.4**
(not rewritten there): they say CES (2006) give the condition "for
stationarity"; the text read gives it for positive definiteness only.

**Correction to §7.3** (not rewritten there): the "independent-normal orthant
value 0.263" was `rho/4 + sqrt(1 - rho^2)/(2 pi)`, which is not the orthant
second moment; the correct value at `rho = 0.5` is `h(0.5) = 0.3045` (closed
form, scipy's quadrature agrees to 1.7e-16). The calibrated `N0` off-diagonal
0.302 sits at it, so "the asymmetric dynamics raise the lower-tail
correlation" is withdrawn. The wrong value was only the fixed-point

### 9.9 What parts 2 and 3 test

anywhere on the grid, in or out of the cone, which is §9.4's spectral radius

definite, beyond 10^6 rows on 62 of 64 paths when only the `(1, 1)` direction
is negative by up to 0.015. **So `a + b + delta g = 1` is a sharp boundary in
the proof and a soft one in a simulation of any practical length**: a fitted
point slightly outside will usually run without a visible failure, which is why
the build must test the bound on the parameters (part 3) and not wait for `Q`
to lose positive definiteness.

**The running variant (the engine's form; not covered by the derivation).**

fixed point of the targeting (§9.3: `mu = Qbar` whenever `Qbar = E R`, `N =
E H`), so a closed-loop simulation has nothing to anchor the level, and it
wanders with the 1/t gain; why it moves down on every point here was not
analysed. On real data the running means are fed by the data and converge to
its moments; this is a property of the closed loop, and it is the one
measurement here that says the running form behaves differently from the fixed
one. It also leaves the cone more often outside (t(5): 1 of 8 at g +0.001 and
a +0.001; 8 of 8 at g +0.01, median 6,384 rows); its intercept moves with the
running means, and why that makes leaving easier was not analysed.

### 9.12 Why the running targets drift (2 series, at the truth)

`drift` (`stationarity_drift.json`; 16 s). **The mechanism.** A running `Qbar12`
is the running mean of `z1 z2`, whose conditional mean is `R12`: it is a
stochastic approximation with gain `1/(500 + t)` of `phi(m) = E[R12]`, the
stationary mean of the correlation when the target is `m`. It therefore follows
`dm/dtau = phi(m) - m`, `tau = log((500 + t)/500)`, and moves down wherever
`phi(m) < m`. §9.3's `mu = Qbar` is about `E[Q]`; the target estimates `E[R]`,
and off the diagonal `E[R12] = E[q12/sqrt(q11 q22)]` is not `E[q12]` (the
normalization by the random diagonal). That is the known inconsistency of DCC
correlation targeting (Aielli 2013, cited from memory, not re-read here).

**The map, measured** (fixed targets `Qbar12 = m`, `N12 = h(m)` the normal orthant
moment, the truth `(0.03, 0.93, 0.03)`, 8 seeds, 98,000 rows after 2,000 burn-in;
median of the time-averaged `R12`):

| `m` | 0.5 | 0.4 | 0.3 | 0.2 | 0.1 | 0.0 | -0.1 | -0.2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `phi - m`, normal | -0.0105 | -0.0100 | -0.0090 | -0.0077 | -0.0067 | -0.0059 | -0.0053 | -0.0051 |
| `phi - m`, normal, `g = 0` | -0.0029 | -0.0028 | -0.0024 | -0.0017 | -0.0010 | -0.0002 | +0.0007 | +0.0015 |
| `phi - m`, t(5) | -0.0144 | -0.0143 | -0.0138 | -0.0133 | -0.0131 | -0.0131 | -0.0136 | -0.0144 |

`phi(m) < m` at every `m` tested with `g = 0.03`. With `g = 0` (symmetric DCC)
the map shrinks toward 0 and has its fixed point at `m = 0` (`phi(0) = 0` by
the symmetry `z2 -> -z2`): the level would decay toward zero correlation.
The asymmetric term adds a negative offset of about 0.006 (normal) to
0.013 (t(5)) at every level, so there is no fixed point in [-0.2, 0.5] and the
level keeps falling. Larger `g` means a larger offset; that is why part 2's
grid points (`g` 0.056 to 0.094) fell faster than the truth.

**The trajectory against the ODE** (the running variant at the truth, `Qbar12`,
median over 8 seeds; ODE integrated from 0.5 with `phi` interpolated):

| rows | 1,000 | 3,000 | 10,000 | 30,000 | 100,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| normal, simulated | 0.423 | 0.391 | 0.417 | 0.403 | 0.392 (range 0.263 to 0.458) |
| normal, ODE | 0.489 | 0.480 | 0.468 | 0.457 | 0.445 |
| t(5), simulated | 0.430 | 0.404 | 0.429 | 0.416 | 0.401 (0.092 to 0.476) |
| t(5), ODE | 0.484 | 0.472 | 0.456 | 0.441 | 0.424 |

From 10,000 to 100,000 rows the simulated fall matches the ODE's (normal
-0.025 against -0.023; t(5) -0.028 against -0.032). **The first 1,000 rows
are not explained**: the level falls by 0.07 to 0.08 there against the ODE's
0.01 to 0.02 (the start from `Q_0 = Qbar`, the running `N` and diagonals, which
the one-dimensional ODE leaves out, are candidates; not tested). So: the slow
drift is settled (the target estimates `E[R]`, `E[R12] < Qbar12` at every
level with `g > 0`, and the drift rate is the map's), the early drop is not.

**Consequence for the boundary.** `delta` depends on the level: with `N12 =
h(m)`, `delta` = 0.536, 0.570, 0.623, 0.659 at `m` = 0.5, 0.3, 0.1, 0 (two
series) and 0.555, 0.614, 0.725, 0.818 (three). As a running level falls,
`a + b + delta g` rises (at `g` = 0.07 and three series, by about 0.005 from
`m` = 0.5 to 0.3): a point inside at the start's targets ends outside, which is
§9.11's paths leaving from "inside". On real data the running means converge to
the data's moments and need not fall; the closed loop has no data to anchor
it. Either way **`delta` must be evaluated at the targets the recursion is
using now**, not once at the start.

### 9.13 The bound the build should enforce, and its message

At every call that sets or updates `(a, b, g)`, and whenever the targets
`Qbar`, `N` the recursion uses have changed (with running targets: at every
row the parameters or targets move, or at least at every update of the
fitted parameters and at every emitted batch), evaluate, on the current
`(a, b, g)`, `Qbar`, `N`:

```text
L     = cholesky(Qbar)                       (refuse if Qbar is not positive definite)
delta = lambda_max(L^-1 N L^-T)              (= lambda_max(Qbar^(-1/2) N Qbar^(-1/2)))
s     = a + b + delta g
admissible  iff  a >= 0, b >= 0, g >= 0, s <= 1 - 1e-4
```

`1 - 1e-4` is the cap the estimators already project onto, so an in-region fit
never trips it; an exact `s <= 1` would admit the boundary, where the
intercept is singular and §9.6's existence argument needs `C_ii > 0` (it
holds there too, but the margin costs nothing). The matrix form,
`lambda_min((1 - a - b) Qbar - g N) >= 0`, is equivalent (§7.1) and could
replace it; `delta` is preferred because it is the projection's coordinate and
its number explains the violation. For a Student-t stage, add **`nu > 4`**:
at `nu <= 4` `Q` has infinite variance anywhere in the region (§9.4), so it is
not covariance stationary; whether that refuses or warns is the coordinator's
call (it does not make `Q` undefined, and the engine allows `nu` down to 2.1).

**The message** (states its reason; cites no plan or record):

```text
ADCC parameters outside the admissible region: a + b + delta*g = {s:.6f}, the limit is 1 - 1e-4
(a = {a:.6g}, b = {b:.6g}, g = {g:.6g}; delta = {delta:.6f}, the largest eigenvalue of
Qbar^(-1/2) N Qbar^(-1/2) at the current targets). Outside it the intercept (1 - a - b) Qbar - g N
is not positive semi-definite, so Q_t can leave the positive semi-definite cone, and the correlation
R_t is then undefined. This can take very long to show: in simulation, paths just outside ran for
10^6 rows without failing. Lower a, b or g so that a + b + delta*g <= 1 - 1e-4.
```

and for the t stage:

```text
Student-t degrees of freedom nu = {nu:.4g} <= 4: the innovations' fourth moment is infinite,
so Q_t has infinite variance and is not covariance stationary. Require nu > 4.
```

### 9.14 What A18 settled and what it did not

**Settled.** The moment recursion's matrix is `b I` (`b^2 I` for second
moments), so `a`, `g`, `Qbar`, `N`, `delta` do not enter it (§9.3, §9.4);
`a + b + delta g <= 1` is necessary and sufficient for `Q` to stay PSD (§9.5);
a covariance stationary solution exists for `a + b + delta g < 1` with `E
eps^4 < infinity` (t: `nu > 4`) (§9.6). The working paper states the condition
as sufficient for positive definiteness only (§9.8). Simulation at 2 and 3
series, normal and t(5), fixed targets: inside, means and variances settle,
the diagonal to its exact moments; outside, no moment grows; `Q` leaves the cone
in hundreds of rows where `C` is negative definite and almost never within
10^6 rows where `C` has one negative eigenvalue (4 of 128 long paths). The
running targets' slow drift: the target estimates `E[R]`, which is below the
target at every level when `g > 0`.

**Not settled.** Uniqueness of the stationary law and convergence from any
start (§9.6; the simulation's agreement across seeds is evidence, not proof);
the running targets' first-1,000-row drop (§9.12); the derivation for
running targets (it fixes `Qbar` and `N`); the published version's exact text
(§9.8); more than 3 series, other `b` than 0.93 and 0.98, more than 8 seeds, more
than 10^6 rows; how the time to leave the cone scales with `s` and with
`C`'s second eigenvalue. The t(5) running-variance figures converge slowly
(their estimator needs `E z^8`), so they show no trend but are not tight.
