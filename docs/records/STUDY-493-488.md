## 2. The state caps (the user's new rules, applied to every figure here)

Every model's data-dependent state needs a stated cap: default **256 KB per
group**, a warning at half (**128 KB**), a refusal at the cap; and a
bank-wide total cap that can be set to infinity. Each model in this record
states its bytes as a function of its shape (`k`, `m`, window, groups), the
check against 256 KB at the shapes run and at the largest `k` or `m`
considered, and the warn point. A bank total is `sum over groups` of the
per-group bytes; with the total cap at infinity only the per-group cap binds.
§6 gives the ETS table and part 4 the ADCC table.

## 3. The design (ETS)

**The model and the estimator** are study 488 §7.1's, unchanged: ETS(A,A,A)
and ETS(A,Ad,A) (and the multiplicative-season forms, which divide by the
updated level as `ETSModel` does), `ETSModel`'s parameters `theta = (alpha,
beta, gamma[, phi])`, one Gauss-Newton step per row from carried
sensitivities, gain `1/W`, projection onto `ETSModel`'s box, a batch start of
`max(500, 3m)` rows fitted by `batch_core.maximize` (study 485's core-held
projected Newton), the buffer replayed at the fit, steps from there. Three
changes, each switchable (`Variant` in the script):

**(a) The admissible region** (Hyndman, Koehler, Ord and Snyder 2008, §10.6:
the discount matrix `D = F - g w'` of the error-correction form has every
eigenvalue inside the unit circle but the one exact unit root, the
unobservable direction). The constraint is

```text
r(theta) = max |eigenvalue of D(theta) after the unit root| - 1  <  0
```

held at every point the batch optimizer tries. `batch_core.maximize` only
knows a box projection, so `maximize_region` in the script is the same
algorithm (projected Newton, curvature from central differences of the analytic
gradient, Armijo backtracking, the same stopping rule) with a projection
`Proj` of a candidate `x` from the iterate `theta` that is the box and then
the region:

1. the box (`project_prime`);
2. if `r(x) >= 0`: a step to the linearised boundary in the metric of the
   curvature `M`: `x <- x - lam M^-1 grad r`, `lam = max(0, (r(theta) +
   grad r . (x - theta)) / (grad r' M^-1 grad r))`, `grad r` by forward
   differences of the radius (`p` evaluations, once per iteration and only when
   needed): it slides along the boundary instead of stopping on it;
3. if still `r(x) >= 0`: 8 bisections of the segment `theta -> x`, `theta`
   being admissible.

The start of the optimizer is the best admissible point of the grid (study
488's 12 or 24 points, and two more deep inside the region, `alpha` 0.01 and
0.05, `b` 0.01 and 0.05, `gamma/(1 - alpha)` 0.001, where a long season's
region still has room). The radius is the largest modulus of the eigenvalues
of `D` (parts 1 and 2 used `np.linalg.eigvals` of a `(m + 2)`-square
matrix; §8.1 replaces it with a polynomial route of `O(m)` memory), `O(m^3)` per
evaluation. For the multiplicative season the additive `D` at the same
parameters is used (the linearisation about unit seasonal factors); a check
(`mul_region_check`) finds the multiplicative filter's monodromy over one
season has radius equal to `radius ** m` of the additive `D` to 3e-9 (m = 24:
0.97964724 against 0.97964724; m = 7: 0.97726883 against 0.97726883). Only
the batch start is constrained: the online steps are not, as the request
says, and the table below reports where the online parameters end.

**(b) The reparametrisation**: `beta = alpha b`, `b` in `[1e-4, 1 - 1e-4]`,
the box `ETSModel` uses (its `beta/alpha` in `[1e-4, 1 - 1e-4]`), with
`gamma/(1 - alpha)` likewise. The batch fit and the online step both run in
`theta' = (alpha, b, gamma[, phi])`: the carried gradient and information are
mapped through `J = d theta / d theta'` (the identity but for the row
`(b, alpha)` of `beta`): `g' = J' g`, `R' = J' R J`, `theta' <- Proj(theta' +
R'^-1 g' / W)`. The stored state is unchanged (the same floats, `theta` kept;
`b = beta/alpha` is derived).

**(c) The Huber score** (part 2). `rho(e) = e^2/2` for `|e| <= c` and
`c (|e| - c/2)` beyond, with the fixed constant `c = 1.345` (the 95% efficiency
constant of a unit-variance normal; the series' innovations have unit
variance and the constant is not scaled by an estimate, so it adds no state).
The batch fit maximizes `-sum rho(e_t)` from the known initial states with the
gradient `sum rho'(e_t) psi_t` (`rho'` the clipped residual) and the
Gauss-Newton matrix `sum rho''(e_t) psi_t psi_t'` (`rho'' = 1` inside `c`, 0
outside); the online step uses the clipped residual in the gradient,
`g' = J' (psi rho'(e))`, and keeps `R` the running mean of `psi psi'` (not
weighted by `rho''`) and `s2` the running mean of `e^2` unclipped, so `sigma2`
still estimates the variance `ETSModel` reports. The oracle is `ETSModel`'s
Gaussian fit: on Gaussian data the Huber estimate is consistent for the same
parameters and differs by its efficiency loss, about
`sqrt(1/0.95 - 1) = 0.23` SE at the constant used.

**Checks** (`checks()`, 3,000 rows): the radius of `D` against an independent
route, the moving-average polynomial of the innovations form (undamped),
`(1 - B)^2 (1 - B^m) y = Theta(B) e` with `Theta = (1 - B)^2 (1 - B^m) +
B (alpha + beta - alpha B)(1 - B^m) + gamma B^m (1 - B)^2`, whose roots'
reciprocals are `D`'s eigenvalues: 9 points at `m` = 7, 24, 168 agree to
within 5.8e-15 absolute in `r` (the points' `r` run from -1.8e-2 to +2.3e-4); the carried gradient in `theta'` against a central difference
of the objective: 7.4e-9 (ETS(A,Ad,A), m = 24) and 1.1e-7 (ETS(A,A,A), m = 7)
relative (squared error), 6.0e-9 and 1.2e-7 (Huber); out of sample (row 1,500
changed by 100): no prediction up to it moved (0.0); the allocated floats equal
the state count (148, both); the constrained fit ends inside the region.

## 6. The state against the caps (ETS)

The user's rule (§2): 256 KB (262,144 B) per group by default, a warning at
128 KB (131,072 B), a refusal at the cap, a bank total that may be infinite.
None of the three changes of this study adds state: the reparametrisation
stores `theta` (`b = beta/alpha` is derived), the region is a check, and the
Huber constant is a fixed number. The state is study 488 §7.4's, checked
against the floats the estimator allocates at `m` = 7, 24, 168 and 1,440, both
forms (`allocation_check`: equal in all eight):

```text
state(k, m) = 8 ((k + 1)(m + 2) + k + k(k+1)/2 + 4) bytes
   ETS(A,A,A)   k = 3:  32 m + 168        ETS(A,Ad,A)  k = 4:  40 m + 224
buffer(m)   = 8 max(500, 3 m) bytes, held until the batch start is fitted
peak(m)     = state(m) + buffer(m)         (both are allocated at once)
```

## 7. Part 3: asymmetric DCC at two series

### 7.1 The model

Cappiello, Engle and Sheppard (2006), on standardized residuals `z_t = r_t /
sigma_t` (the margins' variances), `n_t = z_t * (z_t < 0)` elementwise:

```text
Q_t = Qbar - a Qbar - b Qbar - g N + a z_{t-1} z_{t-1}' + g n_{t-1} n_{t-1}' + b Q_{t-1}
Qbar = mean z z',   N = mean n n',   R_t = diag(Q_t)^(-1/2) Q_t diag(Q_t)^(-1/2)
```

with `g = 0` it is DCC(1,1) (a check holds `r_path` of the ADCC code at
`g = 0` equal to study 485's `dcc.r_path_k` to 0.0). **The admissible set** is
`a, b, g >= 0` and `a + b + delta g < 1` with `delta` the largest eigenvalue
of `Qbar^(-1/2) N Qbar^(-1/2)`: the intercept `C = (1 - a - b) Qbar - g N` is
`Qbar^(1/2) ((1 - a - b) I - g Qbar^(-1/2) N Qbar^(-1/2)) Qbar^(1/2)`, positive
semi-definite exactly when `1 - a - b >= g delta`, and then every `Q_t` is
(the sum of a PSD intercept, PSD `zz'` and `nn'` terms and a positive multiple
of the previous `Q`). That is the condition's role here, and it is derived. It
is not claimed to be the process's stationarity condition (CES 2006 give it
for that, under their assumptions; not re-derived here, and not needed for the
comparison). The estimators project onto it: clip at 0 and, if the sum is over
`1 - 1e-4`, the Lagrange form `theta_i = max(0, x_i - lam c_i)`, `c = (1, 1,
delta)`, `lam` by bisection (the exact Euclidean projection).

### 7.2 The step that was not settled: which `N` the online side uses

The request defines `N` "as the sample mean of `n n'` over the start window".
Study 485's engine carries `Qbar` as the running mean of `z z'` over every row
read (the scan's online targeting). **The online ADCC here carries `N` the
same way, as the running mean of `n n'`**, and the oracle takes the full-sample
means of both, so that online and batch estimate the same `(a, b, g)`: a `N`
frozen at the 500-row start window would differ from the full-sample `N` by its
own sampling error (about `1/sqrt(500)` relative), which the batch fit does
not share and which would show up as a bias in `g`, not as a property of the
online step. That is a reading of the request, not a derivation: a start-window
`N` was **not run**; if it is wanted, the comparison needs an oracle that
fixes `N` the same way. Whichever is chosen, the state is the same (one
symmetric matrix).

### 7.3 The estimator (the A12 engine, extended by one parameter)

`OnlineAdccT` (in `scripts/study_493_488.py`) subclasses study 485's
`OnlineDccT`. Unchanged: the margins (targeted online GARCH(1,1), batch start of
500 rows by the core's optimizer, Huber score 2), the shared start (each
margin's 500 rows fitted and replayed; the stage's start buffer is those rows
standardized by the margins' in-sample variances), the Student-t second stage

### 7.4 The oracle

Written from the definition: arch's Gaussian GARCH(1,1) per margin (`z` from its
in-sample volatility), `Qbar` and `N` the full-sample means, `Q_0 = Qbar`, the
`Q` recursion by `scipy.signal.lfilter`, and `(a, b, g)` (Gaussian oracle) or
`(a, b, g, nu)` (t oracle) maximizing the second-stage log-likelihood by
`scipy.optimize.minimize(method="SLSQP")` from `(0.03, 0.85, 0.02[, 8])`,
`a, b, g >= 0`, `a + b + delta g <= 1 - 1e-4`, `nu` in `[2.1, 200]`, `ftol`
1e-12; sandwich standard errors `H^-1 J'J H^-1` by statsmodels' numerical
differentiation (the second stage only, as study 485 §14, accepted for DCC).
Both oracles reported success on all 200 fits (at most 25 iterations for the
Gaussian one and 63 for the t one).

**Where scipy computes the likelihood.** The per-row likelihood is written by
hand and vectorized (the correlation-stage Gaussian row `-1/2 (ln|R| + z'R^-1 z
- z'z)`, the multivariate-t row of study 485). `scipy.stats` computes it
independently, row by row: `multivariate_normal.logpdf(z_t; R_t)` less the
independent `logpdf(z_t; I)`, and `multivariate_t.logpdf(z_t; shape = R_t (nu -
2)/nu, df = nu)`, at each oracle's optimum, **on every case**. Agreement: at
most 1.8e-13 per row (Gaussian oracle's likelihood at most 5.7e-14, t's at
most 1.8e-13) and 1.7e-9 in the sum over 10,000 rows, over all 200 comparisons. It is not inside the
optimizer's loop: one full `scipy.stats` evaluation of 10,000 rows takes 0.65
to 0.7 s and SLSQP uses about 100 to 300 evaluations (with its finite
differences), minutes per fit, 100 fits per part. So scipy's likelihood
certifies the hand-written one at the point the optimizer reached; it does not
choose that point.

**Checks** (`adcc_checks`, 3,000 rows): `g = 0` equals the DCC path (0.0); the
`lfilter` path equals a direct loop of the definition as written above (7.8e-16);
the start objective's analytic gradient equals a central difference (7.9e-9
relative); the online row score equals a central difference of the row's t
log-density in `(a, b, g)` (8.0e-11 relative); out of sample (row 150 shifted by
5): no emitted correlation up to it moved (0.0); the hand likelihood equals
`scipy.stats`'s on 1,000 rows (3.6e-15 both).

## 8. Part 4: the region check on `O(m)` memory, ADCC at three series, the open items

### 8.1 The region check without the `(m + 2)`-square matrix

Working memory counts toward the bounded-memory rule, transient or not, so the
batch start's region check was rebuilt (`radius_poly`, `admissible`). With
damping `phi` (`phi = 1` undamped) the innovations form is

```text
(1 - B)(1 - phi B)(1 - B^m) y_t = Theta(B) e_t
Theta = S(B)(1 - B^m) + gamma B^m (1 - B)(1 - phi B),
S = 1 + (alpha + phi beta - 1 - phi) B + phi (1 - alpha) B^2
```

(from `y/e = B [alpha + phi beta B/(1 - phi B)]/(1 - B) + phi beta B/(1 - phi B)
+ gamma B^m/(1 - B^m) + 1`; at `phi = 1` it is part 1's polynomial). The
eigenvalues of `D` are the reciprocals of `Theta`'s `m + 2` roots; `Theta(1) = 0`
is the unit root, and `Theta/(1 - B)` (the running sum of the coefficients) has
the other `m + 1`. "Inside the region" is "every root of that quotient
outside the unit circle", decided by the **Schur-Cohn step-down** in `O(m^2)`
time and `O(m)` memory (no companion matrix; each step rescaled by its largest
entry), and the radius is the smallest scale `s` for which the roots scaled by
`s` clear the circle, by bisection to 1e-14 (inside `1 +- min(0.25, 300/m)`, so
no scaled coefficient overflows; a radius outside that band is returned as its
edge). The optimizer's boundary tests (every candidate of the line search, the
start grid) are one Schur-Cohn test each; the value is bisected only for `r` at
the iterate and the gradient's differences.

**Against the matrix route** (`radius_route_check`): 120 random points per
`(m, damped)`, `m` = 7, 24, 168, both forms, alpha, beta/alpha and
gamma/(1 - alpha) log-uniform over the box (many points within 1e-6 of the
edge: 3, 9, 32, 52 at `m` = 24 and 168): largest `|r|` difference 2.9e-13 and
**0 disagreements on admissible or not over all 720 points**.

against 0.25 to 0.61). Two things sit beneath that. A 500-row `N` is a noisy estimate of
the full-sample `N`: the window oracle's fit differs from the full-`N` oracle's
by a median 0.39 SE on `a` and 0.47 SE on `g` (up to 2.5 and 3.5 SE), so the
estimand itself moves with the choice. And the online frozen `N` is not exactly
the oracle's window `N` (max absolute entry difference 0.10 normal, 0.15 t(5)):
the online buffer is standardized by the margins' start-fit variances, the
oracle's by `arch`'s full-sample ones; the 500 rows are the same. So the
third row mixes two differences and the second isolates the estimand's:
against the full-sample oracle the window `N` loses 2 seeds under normal noise
and 3 to 7 under t(5). Not run: the window variant at three series, and a
window of other lengths.

### 8.4 (b) The stationarity condition: not derived

What is derived (§7.1): `a + b + delta g <= 1` makes the intercept `C = (1 - a - b)
Qbar - g N` positive semi-definite for the sample `Qbar` and `N`, so every `Q_t`
is. What is **not derived, and not claimed**: that it is the condition for the
process's covariance stationarity, as CES (2006) state it. The obstacle: the
mean recursion `E[Q_t] = (1 - a - b) Qbar - g N + a E[z z'] + g E[n n'] + b
E[Q_{t-1}]` needs `E[z z']` and `E[n n']`, but `E[z z' | F] = R_t` (the
correlation, with `Q`'s scale normalized away) and `E[n n' | F]` is a nonlinear
function of `R_t` (orthant probabilities for Gaussian noise, others for t). A
bound on how fast `E[n n']` moves with `Q` is what turns the recursion's
stability into `a + b + delta g < 1`; `delta`, a ratio at the fixed point, is
not that bound as far as this study could settle. **Flag for a derivation.**
It is used here only as the estimators' constraint set. Empirically: every
oracle fit (400 across 2 and 3 series) is inside it and none sits on its
boundary, the 100 simulated series per setting stay finite and positive
definite, and the pilot's `E[n n']` iteration converged (§7.3).
