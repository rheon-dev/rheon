# Study 504/507: a counter-based draw stream, contextual bandits and streaming anomaly detectors

A numpy study (2026-11-21, branch `vm/study-504`) for PLAN tasks 504 (contextual
bandits) and 507 (streaming anomaly detectors), and for the draw stream both
need: the user's decision of 2026-11-21 allows random draws on the output path
if they are reproducible, from a counter-based stream whose position is in the
state, keyed on (seed, group, learned-row count), with a seed argument and a
fixed default. Nothing here is built in Rust; every number below comes from

```
python scripts/study_504_507.py            # every table, about 6 minutes
python scripts/study_504_507.py --quick    # the same tables on fewer rows, about 75 s
```

with the code in `studies/study_504_507/` (`philox.py` the generator,
`streamcheck.py` its checks, `bandits.py`, `hst.py`, `rcf.py`). The full run's
output is the appendix. Versions: Python 3.12.3 (Linux x86-64, glibc 2.39),
numpy 2.5.5, scipy 1.18.1, scikit-learn 1.9.1, mpmath 1.4.1, river 0.27.0,
mabwiser 2.7.4, rrcf 0.4.4 (its import needs `pkg_resources`, so setuptools
below 81), vowpalwabbit 9.11.9 (a wheel; it installs).

## 0. Findings

1. **The generator matches numpy to the bit.** Philox4x64-10 written from Salmon
   et al. (2011) equals `numpy.random.Philox` on 3,000 random 256-bit counters
   and 128-bit keys, on every counter of the proposed layout tried (72), at
   numpy's carry boundaries (6), and its uniforms equal `Generator.random`'s
   bits (12 of 12); it and a Philox4x32-10 written alongside reproduce the
   three Random123 known-answer vectors each. Largest gap to numpy: **0**.
2. **The layout**: key = (seed, group hash), counter = (j, lane, n, slot); n is
   the learned-row count before the row, the only position the state keeps (8
   bytes a group). The same draws came out of one chunk, one row a chunk, 1,000
   chunks and 11-29 random chunks, each with and without a save and load after
   every chunk, and of three processes with `PYTHONHASHSEED` 0, 4242 and random
   (one digest in each table; the same consumer keyed on Python's `hash(str)`
   gave three different digests).
3. **Bandits match mabwiser, river and the longhand on the same draws.** Over
   1,500 rows of 5 arms and 6 features, LinUCB, LinTS and LinGreedy in the rheon
   form (rls's square-root state per arm) choose the same arm as mabwiser on
   every row, scores within **2.5e-14** absolute (largest relative 9.5e-10, on a
   score near zero; the longhand with explicit inverses is within 2.7e-15);
   river's `LinUCBDisjoint` the same (9.4e-15) with its tie draws injected. River's "LinUCB" is a different index: `x'β + sqrt(1/β_noise + x'A⁻¹x)`
   with no α, which is rheon's `error_inflation` times the noise scale.
4. **Regret** (2,000 rows, 5 arms, 6 features, noise sd 0.5, 8 seeds; table B5):
   LinUCB α=1 54.6 (sd 9.4), river's index 52.3 (2.6), LinTS α=0.3 58.0 (9.3),
   LinTS α=1 136 (fast root) and 128 (mabwiser's root), mabwiser's LinTS with its
   own generator 134, greedy 184 (sd 215: it can lock on a wrong arm), ε-greedy
   0.1 302, Vowpal Wabbit's ε-greedy 499, uniform random 2,412. With every arm
   changed at row 1,000, LinUCB's second-half regret is 602 undecayed, 153 at
   decay 0.995 and 191 at 0.98 (LinTS 681, 291, 452) (B6).
5. **Half-space trees match river to the bit**, both ways: river's own Mersenne
   Twister draws recorded and served to the port, and the stream's draws served
   to river and the port. Trees (feature, split bits), masses and every score
   equal on four configurations including declared limits. Largest gap: **0**.
6. **rrcf can be matched to the bit, which the oracle scan thought impossible,
   and the match shows that rrcf 0.4.4 does not cut as the paper says at a
   leaf.** Each `RCTree` draws through its own `self.rng`, which accepts any
   object with `uniform`; with the stream served there, a port that reproduces
   rrcf's arithmetic gives every score and every branch to the bit (largest
   gap **0**). But rrcf's insertion, when it reaches a leaf (two thirds of all
   insertions here), computes a span of zero, multiplies its draw by 0, and
   always cuts dimension 0 at `max(leaf_0, p_0)`; 27% of its branches then fail
   the paper's separation, and 32% of its leaves cannot be found by its own
   `query`. The port with the paper's cut has neither defect; its scores rank
   rows like rrcf's (Spearman 0.84) and find the injected anomalies as well
   (AUC 1.000 for both on the full run, 0.9997 against 0.9991 on the quick
   one). The paper's form has no library oracle: its checks are the
   definition's invariants, exact, and the statistical agreement.
7. **Zero-weight rows** (the first included) learn nothing and do not move the
   draw position in any of the three: the state is byte-identical after one,
   and the row is given the draws the next learned row will get.
8. **State**: bandit `8·K·(d(d+1)/2 + d) + 8` bytes (1,768 at K=5, d=8);
   half-space trees `16·T·(2^(h+1) − 1) + 17` bytes with the trees rebuilt from
   the seed at load (81,777 at T=10, h=8; +12 bytes an internal node if the trees
   are kept); random cut forest `T·(ts·(28 + 16d) + 12(ts+1)) + ts·(8d + 8) + 8`
   (1.74 MB at 40 trees of 256, d=8). Caps proposed in 3.5 and 4.6.

For the user: the caps (3.5, 4.6); that the random cut forest is built on the
paper's cut with no library oracle (4.4); the Thompson sampling square root
(2.5); and the normals' one `ln` (1.3).

## 1. The draw stream

### 1.1 Philox

Salmon, Moraes, Dror and Shaw (2011), "Parallel random numbers: as easy as 1, 2,
3". Philox-4x64-R maps a counter of four 64-bit words under a key of two to four
64-bit words, by R rounds of

```
(hi0, lo0) = M0 · c0           (hi1, lo1) = M1 · c2        (full 128-bit products)
c' = (hi1 ⊕ c1 ⊕ k0,  lo1,  hi0 ⊕ c3 ⊕ k1,  lo0)
k' = (k0 + W0, k1 + W1) mod 2^64                           (between rounds)
```

with `M0 = 0xD2E7470EE14C6C93`, `M1 = 0xCA5A826395121157`,
`W0 = 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15`, `W1 = 0xBB67AE8584CAA73B`, and R = 10. It is a
keyed bijection of the counter: nothing else is state, so a draw is a pure
function of its coordinates. numpy's `Philox` is this generator (4x64-10), so it
is the oracle; Philox4x32-10 (the paper's other variant, the one the request
named first) is not in numpy and is checked by its known answers only. In Rust,
4x64 needs `u128` products (portable, no `unsafe`, no platform path): 20
multiplications a block of four words. numpy's C generator takes 9.2 ns a 64-bit
word on this VM (PCG64 4.6 ns): a cost per draw, not per row.

numpy's driver adds one to the 256-bit counter (word 0 least significant, with
carry) before it computes a block, and serves the block's words in order; the
check sets numpy's state one below the block wanted, with an empty buffer, and
reads four raw words (`philox.numpy_block`).

### 1.2 The layout

```
key     = (seed, group_hash)        two u64
counter = (j, lane, n, slot)        four u64
  j     block index inside one row's draws; word i of the row is word i mod 4 of block i div 4
        (a forest puts the tree in the top half: j = tree·2^32 + block)
  lane  what the draws are for, a frozen small integer like ModelState's tags:
        0 policy uniforms, 1 policy normals, 2 a model's construction (half-space
        trees), 3 a cut on insertion (random cut forest)
  n     the learned-row count before this row: rows of weight > 0 the model has learned
  slot  the model's position in the bank (0 alone)
```

* **seed**: a `u64` argument, default 0 (any fixed value serves; 0 is the one
  that needs no explaining).
* **group**: rheon's own `FixedHasher` (`crates/rheon-core/src/hasher.rs`), ported
  (`philox.FixedHasher`) and checked against the two values that file pins
  (`"group-17"` → `0x9c36b9208d671c14`, `7u64` → `0x5201ec7d2d0b0310`), so the
  hash the bank's group maps already use, the same in every process on every
  platform. Ungrouped: 0. If a key hashes by its Rust type, an `i32` key and
  the same values as `i64` hash alike only when non-negative (`write_u32`
  zero-extends, `write_u64` of an `i64` sign-extends), so a cast of the group
  column would move the draws of negative keys: the build should hash a
  canonical form of the key. Two groups whose
  hashes collide (64 bits: about 3e-8 at a million groups) share their draws.
* **n**: kept in the state; it does not count zero-weight rows (1.4). Every
  model that draws keeps it per group; several already keep a learned-row
  count of their own and can share it.
* **slot**: two drawing models in one group with one seed would otherwise draw
  the same numbers; the bank's position is stable when specs are appended, not
  when one is inserted ahead. The alternative is a hash of the spec's name.
  The user's call; the study uses 0.

A row's draws never depend on another row's: a model may use as many as it needs
in a row (LinTS uses `K·d` normals, a random-cut insertion one uniform a level
until the cut separates) and the next row still starts at block 0 of its own `n`.

### 1.3 Words to numbers

| use | conversion | exact? |
|---|---|---|
| uniform in [0, 1) | `(w >> 11) · 2^-53` (numpy's `next_double`) | yes: an integer below 2^53 times a power of two |
| uniform in (0, 1) | `((w >> 11) | 1) · 2^-53` | yes (an odd multiple of 2^-53; one bit more would round) |
| index in [0, k) | `(w · k) >> 64` (multiply-shift, bias at most k/2^64) | integer only |
| standard normal | Wichura's AS 241 (PPND16) of the (0, 1) uniform | 85% of draws: `+ − × /` only; tails: one `ln`, one `sqrt` |

PPND16 is within 4 ulps of the exact quantile (mpmath at 200 bits; mean 0.81),
as scipy's `ndtri` is (4, mean 0.55); the two differ by up to 6 ulps. Uniforms
and indexes are bit-identical on every OS by construction. A normal on the tail
branch (15% of draws) carries the platform's `ln`: bit-identical across OSes
only with a correctly rounded `ln` (task 449's `pxfm`), otherwise it can differ
in its last bits between OSes. **Proposal**: take that one `ln` from the
correctly rounded library whatever 449 decides for the rest, so that a draw is
the same number on every OS. Inverse CDF rather than numpy's ziggurat: one word
a normal (a fixed position), and no rejection loop whose accept test carries an
`exp`.

### 1.4 Zero-weight rows

Chosen: **n counts learned rows only.** A zero-weight row advances the clock and
learns nothing (rule 9); it is still scored, with the draws of position n, which
are the draws the next learned row of its group will use. Reasons: the request's
key is the learned-row count; it makes every output a function of (state, row),
so `ModelBank.predict` (score without learning) gives the same answer twice; and
a zero-weight row then leaves the state byte-identical, which is what rule 9
asks. The cost: a run of zero-weight rows with the same context draws the same
numbers on each (an ε-greedy policy explores on all of them or none). Counting
every row would make each row's draws its own and still be chunk-invariant, but
then the state moves on a row that learned nothing, and `predict` differs from
the emitted score. Checked: G6, B4, H2, R4b.

### 1.5 Checks (tables G1-G6)

| check | result |
|---|---|
| Philox4x64-10 and 4x32-10 vs Random123 known answers | 3/3 and 3/3 |
| longhand vs numpy, random counters and keys | 3,000/3,000 blocks |
| the layout's counters, numpy's carry boundaries | 72/72, 6/6 |
| uniforms vs `Generator(Philox).random` | 12/12 to the bit |
| chunkings (1, 5,000, 1,000, 29 random) × save/load after every chunk | 8 runs, 1 digest |
| three processes, `PYTHONHASHSEED` 0 / 4242 / random | 1 digest (a `hash(str)` key: 3) |
| zero-weight first row | n stays 0, draws of n = 0, then n counts 1, 2 |

## 2. Task 504: contextual bandits

### 2.1 Libraries

* **river 0.27.0** `bandit`: `LinUCBDisjoint` (the only contextual policy;
  one `BayesianLinearRegression` per arm), and the non-contextual
  `BayesUCB`, `EpsilonGreedy`, `Exp3`, `RandomPolicy`, `ThompsonSampling`,
  `UCB`, with `evaluate`. Used: `LinUCBDisjoint`.
* **mabwiser 2.7.4**: `LinUCB`, `LinTS`, `LinGreedy` (ridge per arm, `np.linalg.inv`
  after each fit), and `EpsilonGreedy`, `Softmax`, `ThompsonSampling`, `UCB1`,
  `Popularity`, `Random`. Used: the three linear policies.
* **vowpalwabbit 9.11.9** installs from a wheel: `--cb_explore K --epsilon`,
  used as a regret band only (its learner is hashed SGD with its own reductions).

### 2.2 The rheon form

Per arm a, the state `rls` keeps (square-root information form):

```
A_a = R_a'R_a,   u_a = R_a^-T b_a,   β_a = R_a^-1 u_a          A_0 = l2·I, b_0 = 0
learn (chosen arm a, weight w > 0):   every arm  R ← √lam R, u ← √lam u;
                                       then Givens rotations of [R_a u_a; √w x' √w y]
```

with the diagonal of R kept non-negative (each rotation's radius is `hypot`), so
R is the Cholesky factor of A. Scores before the row's reward is learned:

```
LinUCB (Li et al. 2010)           s_a = x'β_a + α ||R_a^-T x||           (= α sqrt(x'A_a^-1 x))
river's LinUCBDisjoint            s_a = x'β_a + sqrt(1/β_noise + ||R_a^-T x||²)
LinTS (Agrawal and Goyal 2013)    s_a = x'(β_a + M_a z_a),  M_a M_a' = α² A_a^-1,  z_a ~ N(0, I)
ε-greedy                          with probability ε an arm at random, else argmax x'β_a
ties                              lowest index (no draw); or river's rule (2.4)
```

Draws per row: lane 0 index 0 the explore flag (mabwiser draws it for all three
linear policies, so the rheon form does too, to share positions), lane 0 indexes
1..K the random arm's scores (mabwiser takes the argmax of K uniforms; the
multiply-shift index of one word is the cheaper equivalent for a build), lane 1
index `a·d + i` the normals, arm-major as mabwiser draws them.

### 2.3 Against the oracles (B1, B2)

How the draws went in: mabwiser's `_NumpyRNG` wraps a `numpy.random.Generator` in
`.rng`; it was replaced by an object whose `random(size)` serves lane 0 and whose
`multivariate_normal(mean, cov)` serves lane 1 through numpy's own 'cholesky'
transform (`mean + z @ chol(cov).T`, checked to the bit against
`Generator.multivariate_normal` on numpy's normals, 20 cases). River's
`LinUCBDisjoint` draws only to break a tie, through `self._rng.choice`; a
`random.Random` subclass that overrides `random()` alone makes CPython's
`choice` take `floor(r·2^53) mod n` with `r` redrawn above
`(2^53 − 2^53 mod n)/2^53`, and the rheon form's river rule is that arithmetic on
lane 0. Every policy saw the same contexts and learned the driver's chosen arm,
so a difference in a score cannot snowball into a different history.

| policy (rheon form) | against | rows choosing differently (of 1,500) | max abs score gap | max relative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LinUCB α=1 | mabwiser | 0 | 2.5e-14 | 9.5e-10 |
| LinTS α=1, mabwiser's square root | mabwiser | 0 | 1.8e-14 | 8.1e-12 |
| LinGreedy ε=0.1 | mabwiser | 0 | 9.6e-15 | 1.6e-11 |
| LinTS α=1, fast square root | longhand, same root | 0 | 9.3e-15 | 2.4e-12 |
| river's index, river's tie rule | river `LinUCBDisjoint` | 0 | 9.4e-15 | 2.6e-12 |
| (longhand, explicit inverses) | mabwiser / river | 0 | ≤ 8.4e-15 | ≤ 3.1e-10 |

The relative gaps are large only where a score is near zero; the absolute gaps
are about 100 ulps of 1 at most (the square-root state and mabwiser's
re-inverted sums round differently). River broke a tie on 4 rows (the first rows, where every
untried arm is the same), and the injected draws decided them alike.

### 2.4 River's LinUCB is not Li et al.'s

River's bound is `mu + sigma` of the predictive distribution of its Bayesian
linear regression: `sigma = sqrt(1/β + x'Sx)`, with `S = (αI + βX'X)⁻¹`; its
`alpha` is the prior precision, not an exploration weight, and the noise
variance `1/β` sits under the root. At β = 1 that is `x'β̂ + sqrt(1 + x'A⁻¹x)`
with `A = αI + X'X`: the prediction's standard error, which rheon's
`emit_error_inflation` already holds. The index explores less than LinUCB at α=1
where `x'A⁻¹x` is small, and did as well here (B5). A build offering
`policy="linucb"` should say which; the study's names are "LinUCB" for Li et
al. and "river's index" for the predictive form.

### 2.5 Thompson sampling: which square root

Any `M` with `MM' = α²A⁻¹` gives the right distribution; a given z gives
different samples under different `M`. mabwiser (numpy's 'cholesky' method)
takes `M = chol(α²A⁻¹)`: an inverse and a factorisation, `O(d³)` an arm a row.
The square-root state offers `M = αR⁻¹` for one back-substitution, `O(d²)`
(`A⁻¹ = R⁻¹R⁻ᵀ`). Both were built: the compatible root matches mabwiser on the
same z (B1); the fast root matches the longhand with the same root (B1), and its
covariance equals `α²A⁻¹` to 2.6e-15 relative (B2); its regret is mabwiser's
within the seeds' spread (B5). **Proposal**: the fast root, with the oracle test
as here: the longhand on the same z, the covariance identity, and mabwiser's
compatible root behind a test-only switch for the draw-for-draw check.

### 2.6 Regret (B5, B6)

Simulated arms: `θ_a ~ N(0, I/d)`, contexts `N(0, I)` with an intercept,
reward `x'θ_a + 0.5 ε`; pseudo-regret `max_a x'θ_a − x'θ_chosen` summed; 2,000
rows, 5 arms, 6 features, 8 seeds (environment seeds 100-107; stream seeds 0-7).
Table B5 in the appendix has every policy; LinUCB (54.6), river's index (52.3)
and LinTS α=0.3 (58.0) are close together and best; LinTS at α=1 explores too much at this
noise; ε-greedy pays its ε on every row; greedy is fine on average but has the
widest spread (it can lock on a wrong arm); uniform random is the floor. mabwiser
with its own generator lands where the fast-root rheon form does, and Vowpal
Wabbit's ε-greedy (hashed SGD, ε 0.1) above the linear policies. B6 changes all
arms at row 1,000: with no decay the old fit holds on; decay 0.995 and 0.98 (every
arm, every learned row, prior included, as `rls`) cut LinUCB's second-half regret
from 602 to 153 and 191, LinTS's from 681 to 291 and 452: 0.995 is the better
of the two here, 0.98 forgets too fast for 6 features. No library has a decayed bandit: the
decayed forms are checked against the longhand only (B3's `LinUCB, decay 0.99`
runs through every chunking). Discounted LinUCB in the literature (Russac et al.
2019) keeps a second, doubly discounted Gram for the width; that is not built
here.

### 2.7 Chunks, resume, zero weights (B3, B4)

A logged stream (contexts, a uniformly logged arm, its reward, weights 0, 0.5 or
1 with the first two rows zero) through LinTS (fast root), ε-greedy 0.2 and
LinUCB with decay 0.99, in four chunkings, with and without a save and load
(n, then each arm's R triangle and u, as little-endian f64) after every chunk:
one digest of every emitted arm and score per policy. A zero-weight first row
leaves the state byte-identical, and the next row with the same context gets the
same draws and scores.

### 2.8 State

`8·K·(d(d+1)/2 + d) + 8` bytes: R's upper triangle and u per arm, and n. 1,768
bytes at K=5, d=8; 224,008 at K=50, d=32 (B7). Linear in arms, as the scan said
(its `arms·(k+1)(k+2)/2` counts the intercept and a target column in k).

### 2.9 Gaps

* **A stream cannot act.** On logged data the policy's choice and the logged arm
  differ; the build should emit the choice, its score and (for ε-greedy, where it
  is closed form: `1 − ε + ε/K` for the greedy arm, `ε/K` for the others) its
  probability, and learn the logged arm's reward. Thompson sampling's
  probability of its own choice has no closed form; emitting it needs a Monte
  Carlo estimate and more draws a row. Replay evaluation (counting rows where the
  choice equals the logged arm) is river's `evaluate_offline`, not checked here.
* mabwiser's `scale=True` (a running standardiser) was not used.
* VW is a regret band, not an oracle.

## 3. Task 507: half-space trees

### 3.1 How river draws, and the injection

River 0.27.0's `HalfSpaceTrees` builds its trees at the first `learn_one`, from
`self.rng = random.Random(seed)` (CPython's Mersenne Twister), through
`make_padded_tree` (`river/tree/padded.py`), pre-order: at each internal node one
`rng.choices(features, weights=widths)` (one `random()`: `bisect_right` of
`random()·total` in the running sums of the widths) and one
`rng.uniform(lo, hi)` (`lo + (hi − lo)·random()`). So the whole of its
randomness is `2·n_trees·(2^h − 1)` calls of `random()`, in a fixed order.

* **River's draws into the port**: a `random.Random` subclass whose `random()`
  calls the parent's and logs the value (it changes nothing: trees from it equal
  the trees from a plain `Random(seed)`, the last column of H1); the log is served
  to the port's construction.
* **The stream's draws into river**: a subclass whose `random()` serves lane 2
  (n = 0: the trees exist before any row), assigned to `m.rng` before the first
  row; the port takes the same values.

### 3.2 The form, as river has it

Construction (feature `q` chosen with probability proportional to its declared
width, split uniform on the middle 70% of the node's range):

```
q = bisect_right(cumsum(b_i − a_i), u1 · total)
s = (a_q + 0.15(b_q − a_q)) + ((b_q − 0.15(b_q − a_q)) − (a_q + 0.15(b_q − a_q))) · u2
```

then the latest mass `l += w` on the h + 1 nodes of the row's path (`x_q < s`
left, else right; NaN right); after `window_size` learned rows `r = l, l = 0`;
the score before the row is learned, 0 during the first window, else
`1 − Σ_trees Σ_path r·2^depth / (n_trees · window · (2^(h+1) − 1))`, the path
stopping after a node with `r < 0.1·window`. This is river's variant, not Tan,
Ting and Liu (2011) as I recall their pseudocode (from memory, not re-read for
this study): there the dimension is uniform, the split the mid-point of a
randomly perturbed work range, and the score reads the terminal node's
`r·2^depth` alone. The oracle being river, the port follows river.

### 3.3 Against river (H1)

Four configurations (3 features, 10 trees, height 8, window 250; 2, 25, 6, 100;
5, 5, 10, 50; 4 features with declared limits (−3, 3), (0, 1), (10, 20),
(0, 10,000), 10 trees, height 4, window 64), each under river's draws and the
stream's, 2,000 rows with 2% outliers: features equal, splits equal to the bit,
both masses equal at the end, **every score equal to the bit**. Unit weights give
river's integer masses exactly; the port's float sums are the same numbers, and
its adds are in river's order.

### 3.4 Weights, zero weights, chunks (H2)

Weights add to the masses (`l += w`), and the window counts learned rows; unit
weights are river. A zero-weight row learns nothing and does not count toward
the window: the state is byte-identical after it. Four chunkings with a save and
load after every chunk, weights 0, 0.5, 1 and 2 with the first three rows zero:
one digest. **The trees are not saved**: they are a function of (seed, group,
limits, n_trees, height) and are rebuilt at load from `2·T·(2^h − 1)` uniforms
(5,100 at the default), with `+ − ×` and comparisons only, so the same on every
OS. A build that keeps them anyway pays 12 bytes an internal node (H3).

### 3.5 State and a cap

`16·T·(2^(h+1) − 1) + 17` bytes a group (two f64 masses a node; the counter, n
and the first-window flag): 81,777 at river's default (10 trees, height 8),
1,310,577 at 10 trees of height 12, 13.1 MB at 100 trees of height 12 (H3).
**Proposed cap**: `T·(2^(h+1) − 1) ≤ 2^17` nodes (2 MB a group with the trees
rebuilt), which admits the default 25 times over, 25 trees of height 10, or 10 of
height 12. Limits must be declared (or read from warm-up rows): a feature outside
its range falls in an edge cell, as in river.

## 4. Task 507: the random cut forest

### 4.1 How rrcf draws, and the injection

`rrcf.RCTree(random_state=...)` keeps `self.rng`: a `RandomState(seed)` for an
integer, the module `np.random` (the global legacy generator) for `None`. Its
one draw is `self.rng.uniform(0, b_range)` in `_insert_point_cut`, once a level
of the insertion's descent. The attribute takes any object with `uniform`:

* **the stream into rrcf**: an object whose `uniform(low, high)` is numpy's legacy
  `low + (high − low)·u` on lane 3 (counter word 0 `tree·2^32 + block`, n the
  learned rows), served to rrcf and the port alike;
* **rrcf's own draws into the port**: `RandomState(1000 + tree).random_sample()`
  per call, computed as `uniform` computes it and logged (trees equal to a plain
  `RandomState`'s, the last column of R1).

The oracle scan (task 494, §13) took rrcf's draws to be out of reach ("rrcf draws
from numpy's global generator inside its insertion"); that is true only for
`random_state=None`.

### 4.2 rrcf's cut at a leaf

`_insert_point_cut(point, bbox)` builds `bbox_hat = np.empty(bbox.shape)` and
sets `bbox_hat[0, :] = min(bbox[0, :], point)`, then
`bbox_hat[-1, :] = max(bbox[-1, :], point)`. A branch's box has two rows; a
leaf's box is the leaf's point, shape (1, d), so row 0 and row −1 are one row,
the second assignment overwrites the first, and every span is 0. Then
`r = uniform(0, 0) = 0`, the first dimension has `span_sum[0] ≥ 0`, and the cut is
dimension 0 at `max(leaf_0, p_0)`. The insertion reaches a leaf whenever no cut
higher up separates the new point; here that was 19,299 of 30,000 insertions
(R2: 1,500 rows, 20 trees of 128).
Consequences: at a leaf the cut is not random; when `p_0 > leaf_0` the new point
goes right of a cut equal to its own coordinate, where the descent rule
(`x_q ≤ c` goes left) would send it left, so its leaf is unreachable by
`query`; when `p_0 = leaf_0` with other coordinates different, the two points
are not separated. At the end of R2's run 828 of 2,560 leaves could not be
found by `query` (which `find_duplicate` uses), and in R3 135 of 504 branches
(27%) cut between points they do not separate. Scores are still useful (AUC 1.000
on the injected anomalies), because the displacement is counted on pointers, not
by `query`.

### 4.3 Against rrcf (R1)

The port on flat arrays (`rcf.Tree`) in mode `rrcf` (the leaf's box collapsed to
`max(leaf, p)` as rrcf's arithmetic makes it) against `rrcf.RCTree`, 600 rows of
a shingled sine with injected spikes: with the stream's draws, with rrcf's own
MT19937 draws, with 25% zero-weight rows (the first included), and at shingle 10
with 4 trees of 32: every forest score to the bit, every tree's branches
(dimension, cut bits, count) equal at the end. Largest gap: **0**. The forest
loop is rrcf's documented streaming example (forget the oldest point when the
tree is full, insert, score the new leaf by `codisp`, the mean over the trees).

### 4.4 The paper's cut

Guha, Mishra, Roy and Schrijvers (2016): at a node with box `[lo, hi]`, extend
it to `p`, draw a dimension with probability proportional to its span and a cut
uniform on that span; if the cut falls outside the node's box it separates `p`
from everything below and a branch is made there; else descend. rrcf measures
the cut from the top of the span (`lo'_q + S_q − r`), the natural reading from
the bottom; both are uniform, and the port keeps rrcf's so that its draws mean
the same thing. In mode `paper` the leaf's box is the point, so a cut there is
drawn over the two points' spans like any other. Checks, none a library:

* the separation invariant on every branch (left points `x_q ≤ c < ` right
  points): 0 of 504 violated (R3), against rrcf's 135;
* every leaf reachable by `query`: 0 missed (R2), against rrcf's 828 of 2,560;
* the boxes and counts, recomputed from the leaves after every forget;
* against rrcf on the same draws: Spearman 0.84 of the per-row scores
  (identical until the first leaf cut), AUC 1.000 for both on the injected
  anomalies of the full run (R2; 0.9997 for the paper's form and 0.9991 for
  rrcf's on the quick run's 800 rows and 10 trees of 64).

That is the "definition with a seeded tree" kind of oracle, with an exact
library match available only for rrcf's arithmetic. **For the user**: whether
to build the paper's cut (correct, no library to match it draw for draw) or
rrcf's (matched to the bit, but not the paper's tree).

### 4.5 Zero weights, weights, chunks (R1, R4, R4b)

A zero-weight row is inserted with the draws of position n (the next learned
row's), scored, and taken back out; the trees' branches, counts and leaves are
then as before, and its score equals what the same row scores when learned next
(R4b). The first-in-first-out window counts learned rows. Weights other than 0
and 1 have no meaning for a cut tree (a point is in it or not); the build should
refuse them or treat `w > 0` as 1. The score is computed after the row's own
insertion, as rrcf's example does: it uses the row's features and the state
before the row, no target, so rule 2 holds as it does for half-space trees.
Chunkings with a full pickle round trip of the forest after every chunk: one
digest (R4). The trees are a function of the whole history (forgotten points
shaped them), so unlike half-space trees they must be saved.

### 4.6 State and a cap

Per tree, `ts` branches of (dimension u32, cut f64, count u32, box 2d f64,
parent, left and right u32) and `ts + 1` leaves of (parent, count, ring slot u32);
once a forest, the ring of the last `ts` learned points (d f64 and a row id) and
n: `T·(ts·(28 + 16d) + 12(ts + 1)) + ts·(8d + 8) + 8` bytes. 112 kB at 10 trees
of 64 and d=8; 1.74 MB at 40 trees of 256 (the scan's "about 1 MB" left out the
boxes); 7.6 MB at 100 trees of 256 and d=16 (R5). **Proposed cap**:
`T·ts ≤ 2^14` leaves (40 × 256 and 64 × 256 fit) and `d ≤ 32`, at most 9 MB a
group; the default 40 × 256. Dropping the boxes saves `16d` bytes a branch but
makes an insertion walk the subtree for its box.

## 6. Not done, and caveats

* No Rust, no cross-OS run: the uniforms' and the half-space trees' bit identity
  across OSes follows from their arithmetic (integers, IEEE `+ − × /`,
  comparisons), not from a run on another OS.
* The normals' cross-OS behaviour is argued from task 449's measurements of the
  platform `ln`, not measured here.
* Philox4x32-10 is checked by its known answers only (numpy has 4x64 alone).
* Tan et al. (2011) is described from memory where it differs from river (3.2).
* The bandits' off-policy pieces (propensities, IPS/DR, replay) and the
  non-contextual policies (UCB1, Exp3, Beta Thompson) were not studied.
* LODA, the one-class SVM and the other detectors of the scan's §13 were not
  studied.
* Timings are Python's, on a shared VM, and say nothing of a Rust build's cost
  beyond the generator's 20 multiplications a block.
