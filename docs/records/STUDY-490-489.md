## 1. The questions

PLAN 489 proposes sin and cos terms of the row's clock at given periods and
orders, as features any regression in a bank can take. PLAN 490 proposes an STL
or MSTL decomposition over a trailing window, emitted only after an embargo of
`h` rows, so that no output reads a row after its own. Before any Rust:

1. Do Fourier terms computed from rheon's integer-nanosecond clock equal
   statsmodels' `Fourier` and `CalendarFourier` columns to the bit, on even and
   irregular clocks, and which quantity does each oracle compute?
2. How fine is the clock, and how far does the phase drift over a long stream,
   under each way of holding the clock as a double? Where does it matter?
3. Does an STL written from the paper's definitions, run on a ring of the last
   `W` rows, equal statsmodels' `STL` on the same rows, and with which settings?
4. How long must the embargo be against the window, and what does the ring
   cost in bytes? What do the robust option and the iteration counts change?
5. The same for MSTL with two seasons.

## 2. Fourier terms: the definitions and what each oracle computes

For a period `P` (a duration) and harmonics `i = 1..order`, the features of a
row with clock `t` are

```text
sin(2 pi i (t - t0) / P),   cos(2 pi i (t - t0) / P)
```

**statsmodels `Fourier(period, order).in_sample(index)`** ignores the index's
values: it takes the row's position `n = 0, 1, ...` for `(t - t0) / step` and
computes, in this order (`deterministic.py`, `_get_terms` and `in_sample`):

```text
u = n / period            (np.arange(nobs) / period: n converted to f64 first)
x = 2 pi u                (2 * np.pi * u)
term = sin((i) x), cos((i) x)   ((i + 1) * x, then np.sin / np.cos)
```

So it is an oracle on an even clock, with `period` counted in rows.

**The clock form (unreduced)** computes the same from the integer clock:

```text
u = f64(t - t0) / f64(P)        (t - t0 and P integer nanoseconds)
x = 2 pi u;  term = sin(i x), cos(i x)
```

On an even clock with step `s`, `t - t0 = n s` and `P = period s`. While both
are exact as doubles, the quotient is the correctly rounded value of the
rational `n s / (period s) = n / period`, which is exactly what statsmodels'
`n / period` is. So the two agree to the bit until `t - t0` needs more than 53
significant bits (for an odd step, 2^53 ns = 104.25 days after the origin; a
step with factors of two, such as one minute, stays exact far longer). The
remaining operations are identical.

**The clock form, reduced in integers first**:

```text
r_i = (i (t - t0)) mod P   (exact integers)
u_i = f64(r_i) / f64(P);  term = sin(2 pi u_i), cos(2 pi u_i)
```

The angle never exceeds `2 pi`, so its rounding does not grow with the stream.
It is *more accurate* than statsmodels, and so not equal to it: statsmodels'
angle `i x` carries a rounding error of about `eps i x`, which grows linearly
with the row count.

**statsmodels `CalendarFourier(freq, order).in_sample(index)`** is defined on
the timestamps, so it is an oracle on irregular clocks. Its ratio
(`_compute_ratio`) is

```text
start = index.to_period(freq).to_timestamp()       (start of the calendar period)
gap   = (period + 1).to_timestamp() - start        (the period's length)
ratio = to_numpy(index - start) / to_numpy(gap)    (timedelta64 / timedelta64)
```

then the terms at `x = 2 pi ratio` as above. numpy's `timedelta64 /
timedelta64` converts both integers to doubles and divides, in the index's own
unit (pandas 3 parses strings to microseconds, not nanoseconds). The study's
form computes the same with civil-date arithmetic in integers
(`calendar_bounds`): hours and days are fixed counts; a week runs Monday to
Sunday (pandas' `W` is `W-SUN`, weeks *ending* Sunday); months, quarters
(`QE-DEC`) and years (`YE-DEC`) come from numpy's calendar units, so month
lengths and leap years are exact. In pandas 3 the accepted spellings are `h`,
`D`, `W`, `ME`, `QE` and `YE`; `M`, `Q`, `Y` raise "freq is not understood",
and `MS` raises an `AttributeError`.

## 3. Fourier results (part 1)

Settings: statsmodels 0.15.0, pandas 3.1.1, numpy 2.5.5, mpmath 1.4.1, Python
3.12.3, x86-64 Linux. Origin 2026-01-01T00:00Z. Every comparison is of every
column of every row (`part1.json`).

### 3.1 Even clocks, against `Fourier`

| period (rows) | order | step | rows | span | clock form = statsmodels, bit for bit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 3 | 1 day | 20,000 | 54.8 years | 120,000 / 120,000 |
| 24 | 6 | 1 h | 200,000 | 22.8 years | 2,400,000 / 2,400,000 |
| 12.5 | 4 | 1 h | 200,000 | 22.8 years | 1,600,000 / 1,600,000 |
| 168 | 10 | 1 h | 200,000 | 22.8 years | 4,000,000 / 4,000,000 |
| 365.25 | 10 | 1 day | 40,000 | 109.5 years | 800,000 / 800,000 |
| 1440 | 8 | 1 min | 525,600 | 365 days | 8,409,600 / 8,409,600 |
| 60 | 5 | 1 ns | 200,000 | 0.2 ms | 2,000,000 / 2,000,000 |
| 360 | 4 | 10 s + 1 ns | 1,000,000 | 115.7 days | 7,797,660 / 8,000,000 |

The last row is the designed failure: an odd step makes `t - t0` need 54 bits
from row 900,720 on. Every row before 2^53 ns (day 104.25) is bit-equal; the
first mismatch is at day 104.2501, and the largest gap after it is 1.46e-11
(about 66,000 ulps of 1). That gap is the clock's own rounding, not a fault of
either side: statsmodels' `n` is still exact there.

**Accuracy against an exact reference** (mpmath at 50 digits, 401 rows spread
over the stream): statsmodels' columns are off by up to 5.9e-11 at period 24
over 200,000 rows (264,857 ulps of 1) and 3.0e-12 at period 1440 over 525,600
rows; the reduced form is off by at most 4.4e-16 and 9.0e-16 (2 and 4 ulps of
1: the rounding of `2 pi u` and of `sin` itself). The reduced form against
statsmodels therefore shows statsmodels' error, up to 8.6e-11 in these cases.

### 3.2 Irregular clocks

statsmodels' `Fourier` is positional, so for an irregular clock the oracle is
statsmodels on the even grid that holds every row, read at the rows'
positions. On a 1-second grid of 1,000,000 rows (11.6 days), 50,000 rows drawn
without replacement, periods 86,400, 3,600, 604,800 and 1,000.5 seconds,
orders 3 to 6: **bit-equal in all 1,600,000 values**. The same rows through
statsmodels' `_get_terms(pos / period)` equal `in_sample(grid)[pos]` bit for
bit (all 1,600,000), which licenses the next case, where the grid is 1 ns and
too long to build: 200,000 rows at random nanoseconds over 100 days, periods
1 day, 1 h and 1,000,003 ns: **bit-equal in all 5,200,000 values**. Here
`n` is the nanosecond count itself, so the clock form and statsmodels do the
same arithmetic; the case shows the plumbing (integer differences, one
conversion) adds nothing. The reduced form differs from statsmodels by up to
4.1e-5 at the 1,000,003 ns period, where 100 days hold 8.6e9 cycles: that is
statsmodels' angle error `eps 2 pi i n / period`, not the reduced form's.

### 3.3 `CalendarFourier` on irregular nanosecond timestamps

200,000 timestamps at random nanoseconds from 2019-01-01 to 2027-01-01 (two
leap years), plus every month start and one nanosecond either side, the leap
day's last nanosecond and two year-end instants (200,289 rows in all; 200,193
after flooring to microseconds for the microsecond index):

| freq (pandas) | order | ns index: ratio, terms bit-equal | us index: ratio, terms bit-equal |
|---|---|---|---|
| `h` | 2 | 200,289 / 200,289, 801,156 / 801,156 | 200,193 / 200,193, 800,772 / 800,772 |
| `D` | 4 | all, 1,602,312 / 1,602,312 | all, 1,601,544 / 1,601,544 |
| `W` (`W-SUN`) | 3 | all, 1,201,734 / 1,201,734 | all, 1,201,158 / 1,201,158 |
| `ME` | 3 | all, 1,201,734 / 1,201,734 | all, 1,201,158 / 1,201,158 |
| `QE` (`QE-DEC`) | 2 | all, 801,156 / 801,156 | all, 800,772 / 800,772 |
| `YE` (`YE-DEC`) | 6 | all, 2,403,468 / 2,403,468 | all, 2,402,316 / 2,402,316 |

**Bit-equal everywhere.** Two details of the definition:

- *The ratio rounds twice past 2^53.* A year is 3.16e16 ns, beyond 2^53, so
  `f64(t - start) / f64(gap)` differs from the correctly rounded quotient of
  the exact integers in 38,806 of 200,289 rows at `YE` (by 1 ulp; none at `D`
  or `ME`). statsmodels does the same, so a port that is to match it to the bit
  converts and then divides, and does not divide exactly.
- *The index's unit.* The same instants (on whole microseconds) in a
  nanosecond and a microsecond index give bit-equal ratios at `D`, `ME`, `YE`:
  a microsecond multiple has three spare low bits, enough for a year. For
  instants with nanoseconds the two indexes hold different instants, so differ.

**Time zones: no oracle.** A tz-aware index raises in statsmodels 0.15.0 with
pandas 3.1.1 (`TypeError: Cannot subtract tz-naive and tz-aware datetime-like
objects`, after pandas warns that `to_period` drops the zone). A naive
wall-clock index runs, and reads local 23:00 on 2024-03-31 (London's spring
change, a 23-hour day) as 23/24 = 0.9583 of the day, where 22 of the day's 23
hours (0.9565) have passed. rheon reads a `Datetime` in UTC, so a rheon
calendar term is the UTC calendar's unless a local-time rule is stated and
given its own longhand.

### 3.4 numpy's sin against the platform libm

statsmodels calls `np.sin` and `np.cos`; Rust's `f64::sin` calls the platform
libm (glibc here). On 600,000 angles (`2 pi n / 24` for 200,000 rows, uniform
in `[0, 2 pi)`, uniform in `[-1e6, 1e6]`), numpy 2.5.5 (baseline X86_V2,
AVX-512 available) equals `math.sin` and `math.cos` (glibc) in every value. So
on this machine the bit-for-bit claim carries to a libm-based port. Across
operating systems it does not: Apple's libm and glibc differ in the last bit of
some `sin` values, which is the 2026-11-20 libm rule's case (a tolerance in
ulps across OSes, bit-exact on one).

### 3.5 The clock's resolution and the phase drift

The argument error of harmonic 1, in radians, under five ways of holding the
clock, the largest over 16 row counts drawn in each decade of the stream
length (`drift` in `part1.json`, reference exact by `Fraction` and mpmath).
`position f64` is statsmodels; `ns since origin` is the unreduced clock form;
`epoch ns f64` holds the epoch nanoseconds as a double (2026: about 1.77e18,
whose spacing is 256 ns) and takes the phase from the epoch; `epoch s f64`
holds epoch seconds as a double (spacing 2.4e-7 s); `int reduced` is the
reduced form, phase from the epoch.

| rows, period | rows in stream | position f64 | ns since origin f64 | epoch ns f64 | epoch s f64 | int reduced |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 min, 1 day | 1e7 to 1e8 (190 years) | 5.4e-11 | 5.4e-11 | 8.1e-11 | 8.1e-11 | 5.3e-16 |
| 1 s, 1 day | 1e8 to 1e9 (31.7 years) | 1.2e-11 | 1.2e-11 | 2.4e-11 | 2.4e-11 | 6.3e-16 |
| 1 h, 1 week | 1e5 to 1e6 (114 years) | 3.2e-12 | 3.2e-12 | 4.1e-12 | 4.1e-12 | 8.0e-16 |
| 1 day, 365.2425 days | 1e3 to 1e4 (27 years) | 2.1e-14 | 1.7e-14 | 6.1e-14 | 6.1e-14 | 5.6e-16 |
| 1 ms, 1 s | 1e3 to 1e4 (10 s) | 7.1e-15 | 7.1e-15 | **2.3e-6** | **1.7e-6** | 4.3e-16 |
| 1 ms, 1 s | 1e10 to 1e11 (3.2 years) | 7.4e-8 | 7.4e-8 | 1.9e-6 | 1.9e-6 | 7.3e-16 |
| 1 us, 1 ms | 1e3 to 1e4 (10 ms) | 6.1e-15 | 6.1e-15 | **1.5e-3** | **1.7e-3** | 6.2e-16 |
| 1 us, 1 ms | 1e10 to 1e11 (28 hours) | 8.9e-8 | 8.9e-8 | 1.4e-3 | 1.4e-3 | 8.1e-16 |

Every decade is in `part1.json`; the table keeps the first and last of the
short periods and the last of the long ones. What it says:

- **Since an origin, the drift is linear in the cycles elapsed**, about
  `eps 2 pi cycles`: 1e-11 radians after 1e8 rows of a daily season, 1e-7 after
  1e11 cycles. statsmodels and the unreduced clock form share it.
- **From the epoch as a double, the error is fixed by the clock's spacing**,
  `2 pi (spacing / 2) / P`: 9e-12 radians for a daily period, 1.6e-6 for a
  one-second period, 1.6e-3 for a one-millisecond period, from the first row.
- **Reduced in integers, the error is the rounding of `2 pi u`**: under 1e-15
  at every length and period.

**Where it matters.** As features for a regression, an angle error of 1e-11
moves a term by 1e-11, far below any data's noise: for daily and weekly
seasons on minute or second rows, every form is adequate. It matters (1) for
a test to the bit, where only the unreduced form matches statsmodels, and only
while `t - t0` fits 53 bits or the step's spare low bits; (2) for sub-second
periods on an absolute clock held as a double, where the error is 1e-6 to 1e-3
radians from the first row: a rheon form must not hold epoch time in a
double; and (3) for very long streams of short periods, where statsmodels'
own error passes 1e-8. The reduced form, from integer nanoseconds and an
integer period, avoids all three, and its phase is the epoch's, so it needs no
per-group origin in the state at all; its oracle is statsmodels at a tolerance
of `4 eps 2 pi i n / period + 2 ulp` (the scan's proposal, with `n` the
last row; in all nine even-clock cases of §3.1 the reduced form's largest gap
to statsmodels is under the bound, at most 0.32 of it), or an exact
reference (mpmath) at 4 ulps of 1.
