## 1. The question

Scan §1 (item 2) splits each of PLAN tasks 487 (ARMA), 488 (ETS), 492 (GARCH)
and 493 (DCC) in two: a fixed-parameter filter, which is an exact recursion
with a library oracle at rounding tolerance (class E), and an estimator of the
parameters, which online can only be approximate (class A). Every tolerance the
scan gives for class A is a proposal; none was measured (scan §0, §11 step 0).

This study measures them before any Rust is written:

1. Does each fixed-parameter recursion, written row by row in numpy, equal the
   library's batch computation on the same rows and the same start, at the
   tolerance the scan proposes?
2. How far is each online estimator's parameter vector at the last row from
   the library's batch fit on all rows, in the batch fit's own standard
   errors, and how much log-likelihood does that cost, as a function of the
   row count, over fifty seeds?
3. So: do the recursive estimators ship, or do the grid forms (a bank of
   exact fixed-parameter members with a running loss, scan §12 item 5) stand in?

## 2. Rules of the study

- **numpy only for the online estimators.** `arch` and `statsmodels` are
  oracles: imported in the comparison functions and the tests, never in an
  estimator's own code path. Nothing here enters the package or its
  dependency groups.
- **Out of sample by construction.** Each estimator emits its prediction for
  row `t` before it reads row `t`'s target (hard rule 2); a test changes row
  `t`'s value and checks row `t`'s prediction does not move.
- **One row at a time.** An estimator's `update` takes one row; no estimator
  reads a later row. The one exception is stated where it is used: the GARCH
  start buffers `W = 75` rows that emit nothing (scan §8, backcast form (b)).
- **Seeded synthetic data**, generated by the scripts, no data files.
- **Scale equivariance**: scaling the target by `c` must scale the variance
  parameters and predictions by `c²` (GARCH, DCC margins) or `c` (ARMA, ETS
  levels) and leave the dynamics parameters alone; held by a test per
  estimator.
- **Admissibility at every row**: each estimator projects onto its
  admissible set after each step; held by a property test per estimator.
- Scripts: `scripts/study_485/`, one estimator per file, each with a
  `test_<name>.py` beside it, run with the study venv (below). The
  repository's suite does not collect them (`testpaths = ["tests"]`), and its
  environment has no `arch`.

**Environment.** A separate venv outside the repository (the session's
scratchpad), Python 3.13.18, `numpy 2.5.5`, `arch 9.0.0`, `statsmodels
0.15.0`, `scipy 1.18.1` (pulled in by both), `pandas 3.1.1`, `pytest 9.2.0`.
The repository's `.venv` and `pyproject.toml` are untouched. `scipy`, which
comes in through `arch` and `statsmodels`, is accepted as an oracle too
(the coordinator's answer of 2026-11-21): it is the optimizer of the DCC batch
fit, and its `multivariate_normal` gives the DCC per-row likelihood.

## 6. Metrics

Per series (one estimator, one seed, one row count):

- **filter error**: max relative difference of the fixed-parameter path from
  the library's, at the batch parameters and the library's start;
- **parameter gap**: `θ_online(T) − θ_batch` per coordinate, absolute and in
  batch standard errors;
- **log-likelihood gap**: `llf(θ_batch) − llf(θ_online(T))`, both evaluated by
  the library on all rows, total and per row (non-negative when the batch
  optimizer converged);
- **prequential against in-sample**: the online run's summed out-of-sample
  log-likelihood (or SSE) against the batch fit's in-sample one on the same
  rows;
- **path gap**: median relative difference between the online run's one-step
  predictions and the batch-parameter path over the second half of the rows;
  QLIKE difference for the variance models;
- **cost**: state size in bytes and time per row (numpy, indicative only).

Over the fifty seeds: the median and the 90th percentile of each, and the
fraction of seeds inside each criterion.

## 7. Pass criteria (proposed by the scan; this study measures them)

| family | criterion |
|---|---|
| every filter | GARCH and GJR within 1e-13 relative of `compute_variance`; ARMA within 1e-10 of `SARIMAX.filter` after the diffuse rows; ETS additive within 1e-12 of `smooth` |
| GARCH, GJR (G1, G2, G4) | at 10,000 rows, every parameter within 2 batch SEs on at least 90% of seeds; one-step variance path within 5% (median relative) of the batch-parameter path after 5,000 rows; QLIKE within 1e-3 |
| GARCH under t(6) (G3) | reported, no gate: the quasi-likelihood's batch SEs are themselves the robust ones |
| ARMA (A1, A2) | at 5,000 rows, within 0.5 batch SEs on at least 90% of seeds |
| ETS | the recursive form: at 5,000 rows within 2 batch SEs (or, where statsmodels gives none, within 0.05 absolute); the grid form: its argmin equals the batch SSE's argmin over the same grid, exactly |
| DCC | `(a, b)` within 2 batch SEs at 5,000 rows on at least 90% of seeds; correlations within 0.02 of the batch-parameter path after the burn-in |

A family that fails its criterion is reported with the row count at which it
would pass, if any, and the grid form is measured in its place.

## 8. Out of scope

- Any Rust, any change to the package, its tests or its dependencies.
- EGARCH, APARCH, FIGARCH, HARCH and the other `arch` variance processes;
  Student-t, skewed-t and GED likelihoods; estimating a distribution's shape.
- Means other than zero for GARCH (AR, HAR, in-mean).
- ARIMA differencing and seasonal ARMA; Hannan-Rissanen; pseudo-linear
  regression (scan §3 recommends against shipping it).
- Multiplicative ETS, damped trends, Box-Cox, the heuristic initializations.
- ADCC, cDCC, Student-t DCC, `k` above 3, and `pymgarch` (unvetted, scan §9).
- Decay (`half_life`) and `window_size` forms, uneven clocks, zero-weight rows
  and the clock-step rule (scan §12, items 1 and 2): decisions, not
  measurements.
- Real return data: every series here is simulated, so how many rows a real
  series needs stays open (scan §8, "Hard parts").

## 9. Part 1: GARCH(1,1) against `arch`, one series

`scripts/study_485/garch.py` (estimator and comparison),
`scripts/study_485/test_garch.py` (six checks, all passing).

**The estimator.** As in §3, with the start of scan §8's form (b): the first
75 rows are buffered, the start value is arch's backcast of them, and they are
replayed through the recursion, accumulating the Gauss-Newton matrix with the
parameters held at `θ₀ = (b(1 − 0.05 − 0.90), 0.05, 0.90)`, `b` the start value.
From row 76 each row first emits `σ²_t`, then takes one step with
`γ_t = 1/(t + 75)`. State: 16 floats (128 bytes) after the buffer.

**Checks** (`test_garch.py`): the filter equals `GARCH.compute_variance` with
the backcast given and unbounded `var_bounds` to 1e-13 relative (seed 11);
`backcast` equals `GARCH().backcast` exactly; the log-likelihood equals
`arch_model(...).fix(θ).loglikelihood` to 1e-9; a changed row `t` leaves row
`t`'s prediction unchanged; scaling the returns by 37 scales `ω` and every
prediction by 37² and leaves `α, β` (rtol 1e-9); the parameters are admissible
after every row.

**One series:** G1 (`ω = 0.02, α = 0.08, β = 0.90`), seed 485, 10,000 rows

**Against the criterion** (all three parameters within 2 SEs on at least 45
of 50 seeds at 10,000 rows): **1a passes at the boundary (45 of 50), and 3
passes at 47; each changes what is estimated or how it starts (below).**

**Two of the four change what is being estimated or how it starts.** Read
their passes with that in mind:

- **1a and 1b do not estimate `ω` by the likelihood.** `ω` is the moment
  estimate `v̄(1 − α − β)`, and `(α, β)` maximize the likelihood with `ω` tied
  to it: the variance-targeting estimator, whose limit equals the
  quasi-likelihood's only when the model is right and whose standard errors
  differ from the unrestricted fit's (Francq, Horváth and Zakoïan 2011,
  (mem)). The table compares the implied `ω` and `(α, β)` against arch's
  unrestricted fit, in that fit's SEs, which is the comparison asked for but
  not the targeted estimator's own sampling error. arch's `GARCH` has no
  targeting switch to compare against (scan §8). 1a's pass is at the
  boundary (45 of 50).
- **3 is not one-pass from the first row.** It buffers 500 rows, emits
  nothing on them and runs a batch optimizer once; arch stands in for that
  optimizer here, and in the core it would be an optimizer of the model's own
  (scan §8 calls that a separate, larger piece). It also starts from arch's
  own estimator, on a prefix of the same series. The 9,500 online steps after
  it are what the 10,000-row figures measure, and they keep the estimate
  inside the criterion. Its state after the buffer is part 1's 128 bytes; the
  buffer is 500 floats (4 KB) until it is replayed.

**Why the others fail.**

- *1b:* a mean over 75 rows of a series with persistence 0.98 is far from the
  stationary variance, and with `v̄` frozen `ω` cannot correct it; the error
  grows with the row count as the SEs shrink (2.6 SEs median at 10,000).
- *2a, 2b:* a cap relative to the parameter's own magnitude freezes any
  parameter the projection sets to zero, since its cap is then zero. On seed
  485001 the first 20 steps reach the corner `α + β = 1 − 1e-4`, the
  projection sets `α` to 0, and `α` stays at 0 for the rest of the run (the
  plain estimator reaches the same corner and leaves it by its 500th step). A cap needs an absolute
  floor, which this study did not measure.
- *4a:* a gain of `1/sqrt(t)` on a Gauss-Newton step whose per-row direction
  has the variance of the inverse information diverges: `ω` has no upper
  bound and runs off by four orders of magnitude.
- *4b:* a constant gain `c` leaves a stationary noise of about
  `sqrt(c·n/2)` batch SEs, 2.2 at `c = 1e-3` and 10,000 rows, which is the
  median `ω` gap measured (2.1). It is a tracking gain, not an estimator of
  the batch fit, and it does not touch the early-transient failure.

**Not measured:** 1a and 3 together; a cap with an absolute floor; any of
these under G2 to G4, Student-t returns, or past 10,000 rows. 1a passes at the
boundary on one truth, so it needs the other generators before it is believed.

## 12. Part 4: ARMA(1,1) against statsmodels

`scripts/study_485/arma.py` (the estimator, the oracles and the comparison),
`scripts/study_485/grid_arma.py` (the grids: `OUTDIR grid|decay`, resumable,
`--summary` prints the tables below), `scripts/study_485/test_arma.py`.

**The truth** is the coordinator's for this part, `(φ, θ, σ) = (0.6, 0.3,
1.0)`, in place of §4's A1 (`φ = 0.5`); the AR root 0.6 and the MA root −0.3
stay inside 0.8.

**The estimator** (recursive prediction error, as in §3 and the module
docstring). Each row predicts `ŷ_t = φ y_{t−1} + θ ê_{t−1}` before reading
`y_t`, then takes one Gauss-Newton step on the squared one-step error with
the filtered gradient `ψ_t = (y_{t−1}, ê_{t−1}) − θ ψ_{t−1}`, gain `1/W_t`
with `W_t = λ W_{t−1} + 1` (`λ = 2^(−1/h)` under a half-life `h`, 1 without),
and clips `|φ|, |θ|` to 0.99. The kept residual is the a-posteriori one. `σ²`
is the weighted running mean of the squared one-step errors. The start needs
care: at `(φ, θ) = (0, 0)` the two regressors are the same column and the
Gauss-Newton matrix is singular, so the first 50 rows only accumulate the
lag-1 autocorrelation `ρ`, the next 50 build the matrix at `(ρ, 0)`, and
steps start at row 101. Nothing is buffered; every row emits. State: 14
floats, 112 bytes.

**The oracles.**

- *Unweighted:* `statsmodels.tsa.statespace.sarimax.SARIMAX(y, order=(1, 0, 1),
  trend="n")` with its defaults, `fit(disp=False)`: exact Gaussian likelihood
  by the Kalman filter, initialization `"stationary"`, stationarity and
  invertibility enforced, method `"lbfgs"`, `cov_type="opg"` for the standard
  errors (statsmodels 0.15.0). It converged on all 250 cases. The online
  estimator minimizes the conditional (zero-start) squared error, not the
  exact likelihood; the two have the same limit.
- *Weighted, for the decay study:* the definition written in `arma.py`, the
  weighted conditional sum of squares `Σ λ^(T−1−t) e_t²` with zero start,
  minimized by `scipy.optimize.minimize(method="L-BFGS-B")` over
  `[−0.99, 0.99]²`, the residuals by `scipy.signal.lfilter`; `σ²` the
  weighted mean of `e²`. No library computes its standard errors, so gaps
  against it are in the unweighted SEs scaled by `sqrt(n/n_eff)`,
  `n_eff = (Σw)²/Σw²` (1,443 at `h = 500`, 8,656 at `h = 5,000`): an

## 13. Part 5: ETS(A,A,N) against statsmodels

**The truth** (the coordinator's for this part): `α = 0.3`, `β = 0.1`,
`σ = 1.0`, in `ETSModel`'s parameterization, where the trend moves by

(§4's E2 had `β = 0.05`). A local linear trend has no stationary

**The estimator.** Each row predicts `ŷ_t = l_{t−1} + b_{t−1}` before
reading `y_t`, then takes one Gauss-Newton step on `e_t²` with the gradient

(module docstring), gain `1/t`, and projects onto statsmodels' own box

Gauss-Newton matrix over the first 50 rows with the parameters held, and
steps from row 51. `σ²` is the running mean of its own one-step squared

inverse of a complex-step numerical Hessian (the only one `ETSModel`

states are known to both sides, so neither estimates them.

concentrated out of the likelihood: its `σ̂²` is the mean squared one-step
residual at the fitted `(α, β)` (recomputed here and equal to `ETSResults.mse`

here, not the oracle's.

## 14. Part 6: DCC(1,1) with GARCH(1,1) margins

`scripts/study_485/dcc.py` (the simulation, both stages online, the batch
oracle, the comparison and the grid in one file: `python dcc.py OUT.jsonl`,
resumable, `--summary` for the table), `scripts/study_485/test_dcc.py`.

**The truth** (the coordinator's for this part): two series, `k = 2`
(§4's D1 had `k = 3`); DCC `(a, b) = (0.05, 0.90)` (D1 had `b = 0.93`);
`Q̄` with off-diagonal 0.5; each margin GARCH(1,1) at G1's
`(ω, α, β) = (0.02, 0.08, 0.90)`, Gaussian. Burn-in 1,000 rows. With two
series `R_t` has one free entry and the composite likelihood over pairs is
the full likelihood, so the two coincide here.

**The online estimator, both stages.**

- *Margins:* the online GARCH(1,1) of `garch.py` with variance targeting,
  the variant that passed in part 3 (1a). Each row's standardized residual
  uses the variance computed for that row before the row was read. Both
  margins emit from row 76, so the DCC stage sees rows 76 onward.
- *Correlation:* `Q̄` is the running mean of `z zᵀ` over earlier rows (the
  scan's online targeting, form (b)); `Q_t` follows the DCC recursion and
  `ρ_t = q₁₂/sqrt(q₁₁q₂₂)` is emitted before `z_t` is read. Then one
  Fisher-scoring step on the row's second-stage log-likelihood: the score
  `∂ℓ/∂ρ · ∇ρ`, with `∂Q/∂a` and `∂Q/∂b` carried by their own recursion, the
  information `(1 + ρ²)/(1 − ρ²)² ∇ρ∇ρᵀ`, gain `1/(t + 100)`, projected onto
  `a, b ≥ 0`, `a + b ≤ 1 − 1e-4`. The first 100 DCC rows build the
  information with `(a, b)` held at `(0.03, 0.85)`. State past the two
  margins (128 bytes each): 20 floats, 160 bytes.

**What this changes against the batch fit, by construction.** The online
and batch estimates differ in three places besides the step itself: the
margins (targeted online GARCH against arch's unrestricted batch fit), the
standardized residuals (out-of-sample variances against in-sample ones) and
`Q̄` (the running mean of earlier rows against the whole sample's mean). The
gaps below include all three.

**The oracle**, written from the definition (Engle 2002): arch's GARCH(1,1)
per margin (`arch_model(mean="Zero", vol="GARCH", p=1, q=1, dist="normal",
rescale=False)`, as in part 1), `z` from its in-sample conditional
volatility, `Q̄` the sample mean of `z zᵀ`, `Q₀ = Q̄`, and `(a, b)`
maximizing the summed second-stage log-likelihood by
`scipy.optimize.minimize(method="SLSQP")` from `(0.03, 0.85)` under
`a, b ∈ [0, 1]`, `a + b ≤ 1 − 1e-4` (`ftol` 1e-12). The `Q` recursion is
`scipy.signal.lfilter` on each entry. SLSQP reported success on all 100
cases. **arch provides no DCC**: version 9.0.0 has no multivariate model
(its `arch.covariance` is long-run kernel covariance), so no library's DCC
output is used.

**Whose standard errors: an approximation.** No library computes them here.
They are the robust sandwich `H⁻¹ JᵀJ H⁻¹` of the second stage, with the
Hessian `H` and the per-row scores `J` by statsmodels' numerical
differentiation (`approx_hess`, `approx_fprime`) at the batch optimum. They
ignore the first stage's estimation error (no two-step correction), so they
understate the batch estimator's true spread. The robust and the classic
(inverse-Hessian) SEs differ little: the median ratio for `a` is 0.99.

**Checks** (`test_dcc.py`): the `lfilter` path equals the matrix recursion
row by row to 1e-12; the row log-likelihood equals scipy's bivariate normal
log density less the independent one's, to 1e-10; the online score equals
statsmodels' numerical derivative of the row's log-likelihood with the
parameters held (rtol 1e-4); out-of-sample; `(a, b)` admissible and `Q_t`
positive definite after every row; scaling the returns by 37 leaves `(a, b)`
and every `ρ_t` unmoved (rtol 1e-8).

**The grid** (seeds 485000 to 485049; absolute gaps in the approximate SEs;

### 15.3 Caveats

1. **One truth per model**, Gaussian innovations throughout: no heavy tails,
   no second persistence, no roots near the boundary. Every count above is
   for that one point; G2 to G4, A2, E1, E3 and D1 at `k = 3` were planned

2. **Two GARCH passes change the problem.** 1a's `ω` is not a likelihood
