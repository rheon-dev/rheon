A numpy prototype for PLAN task 501 ("Expectile regression ... the asymmetric
squared loss, by recursive reweighted least squares, beside `quantile`") and
for the HyperLogLog half of task 509 ("Count-min and HyperLogLog sketches ...
distinct counts per group, kept only where 494 finds `audit`'s counters and
digest do not already serve"), following task 494's scan
(`vm-tools/research/494-MODELS-SCAN.md` §7 and §15 on branch
`vm-tools/oracle-gap-scan`). Python only, no Rust: the models in `rheon-core`,
`docs/EXTENDING.md`'s places and the measured Rust cost are the tasks proper.
Nothing here is collected by the repo's pytest (`testpaths = ["tests"]`) and
nothing is added to `pyproject.toml`: datasketch is not in the dev group, so
the study runs in a venv of its own.

## Findings at a glance

- `scipy.stats.expectile(a, alpha, *, weights=)` exists in scipy 1.18.1 and
  is within half an ulp of the exact root; an exact sort-based solve is
  within 2 ulps of the data's largest magnitude of it (E1).
- The exact expectile regression (IRLS to a self-consistent side pattern)
  agrees with statsmodels' iterated `WLS` and with `RLM(MQuantileNorm(tau,
  LeastSquares()))` to 2.3e-14 at `tau` 0.1 to 0.9, with decay and with
  zero weights (R1); a window refit each row agrees to 7.6e-15 (R4).
- One pass needs no inner iteration: a row's own update cannot move its
  residual across zero (proved below; R2). It is the decayed WLS to 3e-15 at
  `tau = 1/2`, and elsewhere a statistical approximation within the batch
  fit's own sampling error (R3); without decay, refitting the first 200 rows
  cuts its gap fivefold.
- The refit takes 1.2 to 1.5 passes a row warm-started in a window, 4 to 5
  cold, at most 10 on awkward problems, never the cap; it needs a rounding
  floor in its stopping rule and step halving (R4 to R6).
- Zero-weight rows, the first included, learn nothing and leave no NaN, with
  the one division guarded; unguarded, the first row poisons the state (R7).
- HyperLogLog with `audit`'s own key hash (SplitMix64's finalizer) handed to
  datasketch reproduces `HyperLogLogPlusPlus`'s registers entry for entry and
  its `count()` exactly, `p` 4 to 16 (H1). datasketch's default hash is SHA-1
  (the first 8 digest bytes, little-endian); rheon matches it bit for bit
  only by hashing with SHA-1 (H2), which is not recommended.
- The error is the paper's `1.04/sqrt(m)` from `p = 6` up, over 200 trials a
  precision (H4); merge is the exact register-wise maximum and equals the
  sketch of the union (H7); 2^p bytes a column (16 B at `p = 4`, 256 B at 8,
  4 kB at 12, 16 kB at 14).
- One decision for the user: whether a zero-weight row's value is counted
  (`audit`'s exact counter counts it today) (H9).

## Part A. Expectiles (task 501)

### The quantity

At level `tau` in `(0, 1)` the expectile of a weighted sample is the `t` with

```text
tau * sum_i w_i (y_i - t)_+  =  (1 - tau) * sum_i w_i (t - y_i)_+
```

(Newey and Powell 1987), the minimiser of `sum_i w_i a(y_i - t) (y_i - t)^2`
with the asymmetric weight

```text
a(r) = tau        if r > 0
     = 1 - tau    if r <= 0          (scipy's side at r = 0)
```

At `tau = 1/2` it is the weighted mean. Expectile *regression* replaces `t`
by `z'beta` (`z` the regressors, the constant first, `p` of them) and
minimises `L(beta) = sum_i v_i a(r_i) r_i^2`, `r_i = y_i - z_i'beta`, `v_i`
the row's weight decayed to now. `L` is convex, piecewise quadratic and C1
(its gradient `-2 sum v a r z` is continuous at `r = 0`), so for a fixed
assignment of sides the minimiser is a weighted least-squares fit, and the
minimiser of `L` is the WLS fit whose sides agree with its own residuals:

```text
sum_i v_i a(r_i) r_i z_i = 0          (the stationarity condition)
```

Unlike `quantile`, no smoothing band is needed (the loss already has
curvature everywhere, between `min(tau, 1 - tau)` and `max(tau, 1 - tau)`
times least squares), so the model needs no residual scale, no band and no
warm-up rule of three rows a coefficient: a weight is bounded, which is the
scan's point that `quantile`'s review finding N9 (an unbounded secant weight)
cannot arise.

### Oracles, checked to exist in the installed versions

- **scipy 1.18.1** has `scipy.stats.expectile(a, alpha=0.5, *, weights=None,
  axis=None)` (`scipy/stats/_stats_py.py`). It solves the first-order
  condition above with `scipy.optimize.elementwise.find_root` (Chandrupatla's
  bracketing method, bracket `[min a, max a]`) at the default tolerances.
  Checked in rationals (`fractions.Fraction`) on six 10,000-row cases with
  decayed weights: scipy's root is within 0.44 ulp of the exact root.
- **statsmodels 0.15.0**: `WLS` iterated by hand (the sides from the last
  fit's residuals, refit, until no side changes; statsmodels' own `pinv`
  solve, not our normal equations), and `RLM(endog, exog,
  M=MQuantileNorm(tau, LeastSquares()))` (`statsmodels/robust/norms.py`,
  "Expectiles are M-quantiles with the LeastSquares as base norm"), run at
  `tol=1e-14`. `RLM` takes no weights; the loss is homogeneous of degree 2,
  so rows scaled by `sqrt(v)` (the constant column too) give the weighted fit.
- **The WLS longhand**: our own normal equations (`expectile.wls`, Cholesky),
  and the stationarity condition evaluated directly.

### The forms prototyped

**One pass** (`OnlineExpectile`; the scan's "A in one pass"). The row's side
is judged by its residual against the fit before it (the prediction, out of
sample by rule 2); the row then enters decayed mean-form sums at weight
`w a`:

```text
pred = z'beta                 r = y - pred        a = a(r)  (1/2 before any fit)
S'   = lam S + w a            (the asymmetric weight, decayed)
W'   = lam W + w              (n_eff: the raw weight, rule 8; emitted as weight_sum)
if w a == 0: S <- S', W <- W', nothing else (rule 9: advance the clock, learn nothing)
f    = w a / S'
G'   = G + f (z z' - G)       (mean Gram)
c'   = c + f (z y - c)        (mean cross-moment)
beta = G'^-1 c'               (once p rows of positive weight are in and G' is positive definite)
```

**A row's update cannot move its own residual across zero**, so the one pass
needs no inner iteration on its row. From `S'G' beta' = lam S G beta + w a z y`
and `S G beta = S c`, `beta' - beta = w a (S'G')^-1 z r`, so

```text
r_post = r (1 - w a h),   h = z'(S'G')^-1 z,   0 <= w a h <= 1
```

because `S'G'` holds `w a z z'`. The residual after the row keeps the sign of
the one before it, or reaches 0 when the row is interpolated (its weight then
does not matter), whatever `lam` is. Judging the side after the row would
pick the same side (table R2: 0 of 29,960 rows differ). What one pass cannot
do is re-judge an *earlier* row whose side the fit has since crossed; that
needs the rows, which the refit and the window keep.

**The exact refit** (`irls`): over kept rows with weights `v`, start from
the sides at a given fit (warm) or from least squares (cold); a *pass* is one
WLS solve at the current sides; re-judge every side; repeat. It stops:

1. when no side changes after a full step: an exact fixed point, the
   minimiser of `L` (the normal end: table R6, 95% and up of the awkward
   problems, every one of the ordinary ones);
2. when the fitted values moved by no more than `max(1e-12 * s, 8 eps
   max|fitted|)` (`s` the weighted residual RMS): rows whose residual sits
   at rounding level trade sides with no effect on the fit, and an exact fit
   (`s = 0`) must not read as one that still moves;
3. when a side pattern comes back with no lower loss than it had (a cycle of
   such rows; the lowest loss met is kept);
4. after `max_iter` passes (50 here; `quantile`'s `REFIT_PASSES` is 200),
   counted as a solve failure, the lowest-loss iterate kept.

A full Newton step on a piecewise quadratic can overshoot: a step that does
not lower `L` (past its rounding) is halved, which a descent direction of a
convex `L` always allows. At `tau = 1/2` one solve is the answer.

Two uses of the refit were prototyped:

- **The first rows refit** (`run_online(refit_rows=R)`), as
  `quantile_refit_rows` does for `quantile`: when the stream reaches its
  `R`-th row, the buffered rows are refit exactly (decayed weights), the
  one-pass sums are rebuilt row by row with each side judged at the
  converged fit, and the stream goes on in one pass.
- **The window** (`run_window`): the last `N` rows (decayed by `lam` inside
  the window), refit on every row warm-started from the previous row's fit.
  The prediction for row `t` is the fit of the window before it (rule 2).
  The window's `n_eff` is the weight inside it (rule 8). The scan said "no
  window (as `quantile`)"; this shows a window is exact and cheap in passes,
  at the cost of the ring.

One pass against the exact decayed fit of every row: the RMS of the
fitted-value gap in units of the batch fit's residual scale, beside a
sampling yardstick `sqrt(p / n_kish)` (`n_kish = (sum v)^2 / sum v^2`), the
size of the batch fit's own sampling error in the same units. At `tau = 1/2` one pass is
the decayed WLS to rounding (also E3). Away from it, one pass is a
statistical approximation, never worse than the batch fit's own sampling
error in these runs and mostly far under it: with decay (`lam = 0.999`,
`0.99`) the mean gap is 0.02 to 0.4 of the yardstick and the largest 1.2
(`lam = 0.99`, `n = 500`, `tau = 0.9`); the rows judged on the wrong side
early decay away, so the gap reaches a floor set by `lam`, not by `n`.
Without decay the early misjudged rows stay at full weight and leave only by
dilution: at `n = 500`, `tau = 0.9` the mean gap is 1.0 yardstick, and
**refitting the first 200 rows exactly cuts it fivefold** (0.102 to 0.020),
which is the case for `expectile_refit_rows` beside `quantile_refit_rows`.
Under decay the refit buffer has decayed away by the time it matters, and
the refit changes nothing at `n >= 5,000`.

### Convergence: what the Rust refit should do

- **One pass: one solve a row, no inner iteration.** The row's side cannot
  change with its own update (the identity above; R2), so iterating on the
  row is a no-op. The approximation is in the earlier rows, not this one.
- **Refit and window: iterate to the exact fixed point.** A pass is one WLS
  solve at the current sides. Measured: a warm start (sides at the fit
  before the row) takes 1.2 to 1.5 passes a row on average in a window
  (R4; p99 3, max 5; `tau = 1/2` always 1), about 2 when the window moves 7
  rows between refits (R5, max 4), and a cold start from least squares 3.7
  to 4 (max 5). At 2,000 rows a full refit takes 1 to 5 passes (R1). On the
  awkward problems of R6 the largest count is in the table; none reached the
  cap of 50.
- **Tolerance.** Primary: no side changes after a full step (exact; the
  answer is the minimiser to rounding). Secondary: the fitted values moved
  by at most `max(1e-12 s, 8 eps max|fitted|)`; `huber`'s and `quantile`'s
  refits use `REFIT_TOL = 1e-10` of the scale with the same rounding floor,
  and the expectile can share it. Without the rounding floor an exactly
  interpolated problem (integer data on a line, `s = 0`) cycled through
  three side patterns for 40 passes before stopping, found in the first
  stress run; with it, and with the no-progress cycle rule, it stops on the
  first repeat.
- **Step control.** A full step raised the loss on a few percent of refits
  at extreme levels (R6, `tau = 0.01/0.99`: halvings on 14% of problems;
  R4: 2 to 21 halvings over 6,000 refits a cell). Halving to a lower loss
  always ended the descent at the same fixed point. This mirrors
  `quantile_refit`'s halving and should reuse it.
- **When it does not converge** (the cap; never seen here): count it in the
  model's `solve_failures`, as `quantile`'s and `huber`'s refits do, keep the
  lowest-loss iterate met (its fit is still a descent from the warm start,
  so never worse than the fit before the row), and go on: the next row's
  refit starts from it, so the window retries on its own. A refit with fewer
  than `p` positive-weight rows is no fit (no solve, nothing emitted), not a
  failure.

### Zero-weight rows (rule 9)

A zero-weight row advances the clock and learns nothing. In the one pass,
`w a = 0` skips the mean-form update after `S` and `W` have been decayed;
without the guard, a first row of weight 0 makes `f = 0/0` and the state is
NaN for ever (R7's second line). With it, a stream that opens with three
zero-weight rows emits nothing for them, and its fit and every later output
are bit-identical to the stream without them, at `lam = 1` and `lam = 0.99`
(the sums are 0 and decay to 0). Mid-stream, the fit does not move across
ten zero-weight rows while `n_eff` decays by `lam` each. In the window, rows
of weight 0 hold slots and add nothing; a window whose rows all weigh 0 has
no fit and emits nothing (no NaN in the state), and the first prediction
comes on the row after `p` rows of positive weight are in. In the refit, the
WLS of a weight vector with fewer than `p` positive entries is no fit
(`chol_solve` refuses), never a 0/0. `n_eff` (the raw weight) and the
asymmetric weight `S` are different numbers: `min_weight` should read `W`,
the same accumulated weight every model emits (rule 8), not `S`.

### State size

Per target, with `p` coefficients (`k + 1`, the constant included), as
doubles of 8 bytes:

| form | doubles | k = 5 (p = 6) | k = 20 (p = 21) |
|---|---|---|---|
| one pass: `G` (upper), `c`, `beta`, `S`, `W`, rows | `p(p+1)/2 + 2p + 3` | 36 (288 B) | 276 (2,208 B) |
| + first-rows refit buffer, until row `R` | `+ R (p + 1)` (`x`, `y`, `w`) | `R = 200`: +1,400 (11.2 kB), freed after | +4,400 (35.2 kB) |
| + window of `N` rows | `+ N (p + 1)` (one more a row for a clock step under a time decay) | `N = 1,000`: +7,000 (56 kB) | +22,000 (176 kB) |

So `8 (p(p+1)/2 + 2p + 3 + N (p + 1))` bytes with a window of `N`. Sides
need no storage (they are re-judged at the stored fit). `quantile`'s state
carries, besides this, a residual scale (an `EwQuantile` per target for the
MAD) and the band's weights; the expectile needs none of them, so "as
`quantile`" (the scan) is an upper bound. A window's per-row cost is
`passes * O(N p^2)` for the Grams rebuilt from the ring, plus `O(p^3)` for
the solves; keeping the two side Grams and moving a row between them when
it flips is `O(flips p^2)` but a downdate loses bits, so a rebuild keeps the
fit a function of the window's rows alone.

### Gaps and caveats (Part A)

- **The window is new relative to the scan**, which said "no window (as
  `quantile`)". A window here costs `N (p + 1)` doubles a target and
  `passes * N p^2` a row; it is exact (the scan's E) and its passes are
  measured. Whether to offer it is a state-size question for the user
  (CLAUDE.md, "State stays bounded"): bounded by `N`, about 56 kB a target
  at `k = 5`, `N = 1,000`.
- **Warm-up.** Before any fit the prototype weighs a row `1/2` (least
  squares), as `quantile`'s warm-up does; the first-rows refit re-judges
  those rows. `p` rows of positive weight are needed for a fit (a
  Cholesky pivot under `1e-12` of the largest diagonal is no fit); `ridge`
  and `standardize` were not prototyped (`quantile`'s code carries them, and
  the expectile's stationarity condition gains the ridge term as
  `TestTheQuantileFitsDefinition` does).
- **One Gram per level.** The weights differ by level, so several levels in
  one spec need one set of sums each (the scan's point).
- **Extreme levels.** At `tau = 0.01` or `0.99` one side carries 99% of the
  weight and the effective sample on the other side is small; the refit
  converged in at most 7 passes but halved its step on 14% of problems.
  Reporting the per-side weight (or warning below a count) is a cheap guard.
- **Speed.** The prototype is numpy, row by row; its timings say nothing
  about Rust. The Rust task measures the one pass (one rank-one update and
  one `p x p` solve a row, as `ewridge`) and the window.
- **Chunk invariance (rule 3)** holds by construction: every form is row by
  row, and the refit's trigger is a row count. Not separately tested here.


## Part B. HyperLogLog distinct counts for `audit` (task 509)

The scan's verdict, kept: heavy hitters are `audit`'s Misra-Gries counters
already; a distinct count past `distinct_cap` is the gap, and HyperLogLog
fills it as an extension of `audit`, undecayed and unwindowed (a register is
a maximum and cannot fade). Count-min was not studied here: datasketch has
none (the scan's correction 1), and its use is the per-row key count, a
separate accumulator.

### Keys, hash, registers, estimate

**Keys.** `audit` keys a value by its f64 bits with `-0` read as `0`
(`audit.rs`, `key`); a NaN keeps its payload, so the null the plumbing writes
(`0x7FF8_0000_004E_554C`) counts apart from a NaN in the data. The sketch
takes the same key, so below the cap (exact) and past it (the sketch) the
count is of the same thing (H8).

**Hash.** `h = splitmix64_finalizer(key)`, which is `audit`'s
`KeyHasher::write_u64` already (the counters' table hash):

```text
z = key + 0x9e3779b97f4a7c15     (mod 2^64)
z = (z ^ (z >> 30)) * 0xbf58476d1ce4e5b9
z = (z ^ (z >> 27)) * 0x94d049bb133111eb
h = z ^ (z >> 31)
```

Its constants are in the source; no seed is drawn at run time, so the same
value lands in the same register in every process on every platform (H10:
one register digest under four `PYTHONHASHSEED`s, in fresh processes; and
the pinned values `splitmix(0) = 0xe220a8397b1dcdaf`, SplitMix64's first
output from state 0, and `hasher.rs`'s own pin `FixedHasher(7u64) =
0x5201ec7d2d0b0310`, reproduced by the Python port). It is a bijection of
64-bit words, so two distinct keys never share a hash. The scan asked for its
avalanche on sequential integers before trusting it (H3, H6).

**Registers** (datasketch's layout, so its classes take this hash and give
the same array): `m = 2^p` one-byte registers; a value goes to register
`j = h & (m - 1)` and offers `rho = (64 - p) - bitlen(h >> p) + 1`, the
position of the leftmost 1 in the `64 - p` bits left; the register keeps the
largest offer. A maximum cannot depend on order or chunking (rule 3: H7's
977 shuffled chunks), and two sketches merge by the register-wise maximum.

**Estimate.** Plain HyperLogLog (Flajolet, Fusy, Gandouet and Meunier 2007):
`E = alpha_m m^2 / sum_j 2^(-M_j)`, and under `2.5 m` linear counting
`m ln(m / V)` (`V` the empty registers) while `V > 0`; with a 64-bit hash no
large-range correction. HLL++'s dense estimate (Heule, Nunkesser and Hall
2013; datasketch's `HyperLogLogPlusPlus.count`): linear counting while it is
under the precision's threshold, else `E` minus the bias interpolated from
the paper's tables (the mean over the six nearest raw estimates) while
`E <= 5 m`, else `E`. The relative standard error is about `1.04/sqrt(m)`.

### datasketch: which hash, and can rheon match it bit for bit

datasketch 2.0.0's `HyperLogLog(p=8, reg=None, hashfunc=sha1_hash32)` and
`HyperLogLogPlusPlus(p=8, reg=None, hashfunc=sha1_hash64)` (`p` from 4 to
16). The defaults are SHA-1: `sha1_hash32(data)` is the first 4 bytes of
`hashlib.sha1(data).digest()` read little-endian (`struct.unpack('<I', ...)`),
`sha1_hash64` the first 8 (`'<Q'`), over the bytes the caller passes (here
the key's 8 little-endian bytes). Both classes call `hashfunc` on whatever
`update` is given, so any hash can be handed in.

- **With rheon's hash handed in** (`hashfunc=splitmix`): the registers are
  equal entry for entry and `count()` is equal *exactly* (0 relative gap),
  at `p = 4, 8, 12, 14, 16`, on random, sequential-integer and cent-grid
  values (H1). Against the plain `HyperLogLog` (a 32-bit hash) the sketch
  is fed `h`'s top 32 bits and matches too. This is the oracle the scan
  proposed, and it needs the hash from Python: a five-line port (above), or
  the Rust function exposed to tests.
- **With datasketch's default** (no `hashfunc`): rheon matches bit for bit
  only by hashing with SHA-1 over the key's 8 little-endian bytes, which the
  prototype does (H2: registers equal to the default's, the count equal).
  In Rust that is a SHA-1 implementation, a new crate (RustCrypto's `sha1`,
  pure Rust, statically linked: rule 12 asks that it be raised first) or a
  hand-written one, and a cryptographic compression of a 64-byte block per
  value where splitmix is two multiplies (memory: on the order of a hundred
  times the cost). Nothing in the estimate needs it: the error band is the
  same (H4, H6). **Recommendation: splitmix (the key hasher `audit` already
  runs), handed to datasketch in the tests.**

squared distances, first six) needs a stated tie rule in Rust; ties between
table entries at equal distance from `E` did not occur in these runs, but a
stable sort by (distance, index) is the rule to write down.

**Zero-weight rows: a conflict to settle before the build.** `audit`'s
docstring says a value is a value whatever its row weighs: "a row of weight
0 is counted like any other (hard rule 9 asks only that it divide nothing by
zero, and nothing here divides by a weight)", and its exact counter counts
it. The coordinator's reading of rule 9 for this study is that a
zero-weight row learns nothing, so a sketch would skip it. Both are a line
of code (`HLL.update(..., count_zero_weight=)`); H9 shows they count
different things on a stream with every third row at weight 0. A sketch that
skipped them would continue, past the cap, an exact counter that counted
them, so the distinct count would change meaning at the cap.
**Recommendation: follow `audit`'s convention (count them), or change the
counter and the sketch together; the user's call.** Either way nothing
divides by a weight, so neither form can make a NaN.

### Gaps and caveats (Part B)

- **Polars and the keys.** The truth here is `np.unique` on `audit`'s keys.
  Polars' `n_unique` on a float column treats `-0.0` and `0.0` as one value
  and every NaN as one value (memory: to be checked in the test); `audit`
  keeps NaN payloads apart, so the test's truth must be computed on the keys
  (a `reinterpret` to `u64` with `-0` mapped to `0`), not on the floats.
  Integer columns reach `audit` as f64: integers above `2^53` that round to
  one double are one key, in the exact counter and in the sketch alike.
- **The hash is frozen with the state.** Registers live in saved states, so
  the hash and the register layout are part of the schema (rule 5); a change
  of either is a layout change with fixtures.
- **datasketch's plain `HyperLogLog.count()` divides by zero** when the raw
  estimate is under `2.5 m` and no register is empty (`m * log(m / 0)`,
  `ZeroDivisionError`): 88 of 7,000 sketches at `p = 4` with 10 to 80 values.
  Flajolet et al. return the raw estimate there, as the prototype does;
  HLL++'s `count()` does not have the bug. A test against the plain class
  must stay out of that region; the HLL++ class is the oracle to use anyway.
- **`p` range.** datasketch takes `p` from 4 to 16; HLL++'s tables run to 18.
  Propose `p` from 8 to 16 for `audit` (default 12: 4 kB, 1.6%), `p < 8`
  having a spread above the formula and a value no one should read.
- **Not studied**: count-min (no datasketch class; the scan names Apache
  `datasketches` and river as its oracles), HLL's sparse form, a decayed or
  windowed distinct count (no oracle), and the Rust cost (a hash, a shift, a
  `leading_zeros` and a byte max per value: the Rust task measures it).
