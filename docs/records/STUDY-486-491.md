## 1. AR(p): the question

Can an online AR(p), `y_t = c + Σ_{i=1..p} φ_i y_{t−i} + e_t`, be fit (a) by
exponentially weighted least squares on a ring of `p` lags and (b) by
Yule-Walker on running autocovariances, and match statsmodels `AutoReg` (OLS)
and `yule_walker` to floating precision with no decay, and the batch weighted
fit under decay?

**Answer: yes, both, with two conventions stated below** (the Yule-Walker
weights must taper the sequence, not weight the pair by its later row; and the
sums are best kept about an origin). The gap to the library is 1e-14 relative at
`p = 1` and at most 4.1e-11 (least squares) and 3.7e-11 (Yule-Walker) at
`p = 20`, `N = 20000`, no decay; under decay at most 1.2e-11 and 1.2e-12.

## 2. The math

### 2a. Exponentially weighted least squares on the lag ring

The state keeps a **ring of the last `p` learned values**. A row is learned when
its weight `w_t > 0` and the ring already holds `p` values; the first `p`
learned rows only fill the ring (`AutoReg`'s `hold_back = p`). With
`z_t = (1, y_{t−1}, …, y_{t−p})` read from the ring:

```
pred_t = z_t' β_{t−1}                          out of sample: before the update (hard rule 2)
G_t    = λ G_{t−1} + w_t z_t z_t'              k×k, k = p + 1
b_t    = λ b_{t−1} + w_t z_t y_t
W_t    = λ W_{t−1} + w_t                       n_eff before this row: W_{t−1} (hard rule 8)
β_t    = G_t⁻¹ b_t                             Cholesky, then two triangular solves
```

The solve's guard: `β` is undefined (NaN, no prediction) until `k` rows have
been learned, and whenever the Cholesky factorization of `G_t` fails (not
positive definite). Every row decays the state on the clock (`λ` per row,
including zero-weight and missing rows); only a learned row adds to it.

**The origin.** The sums are kept about `o`, the first learned value:
`y ↦ y − o` before anything enters the ring or the sums. Least squares with an
intercept is shift-equivariant, so the coefficients on the original scale are
`φ` unchanged and `c = c' + o (1 − Σ φ_i)`, and the prediction adds `o` back.
This cuts the gap to `AutoReg` by 2.5 to 6 times at `N = 20000` (section 4);
it costs 8 bytes.

This is the sum form. The mean form that `ewridge` uses
(`a = λW/(λW + w)`, `b = w/(λW + w)`) meets `0/0` on a zero-weight first row;
the sum form never divides, and the guard there is to skip the update when
`λW + w = 0` (hard rule 9). A zero-weight row adds nothing here: no `0·y`
product is formed, so a NaN target cannot reach the state. Its only effect is
the decay, and since `G` and `b` scale together, `β` is unchanged and no solve
is needed on it.

### 2b. Yule-Walker on running autocovariances

Each learned row `s` has the weight `W_s = w_s λ^{age_s}` (its age on the clock,
counting every row) and the amplitude `a_s = √W_s`. The autocovariances are
those of the **tapered, demeaned sequence** `u_s = a_s (y_s − m)`, with the
final weighted mean `m = Σ W_s y_s / Σ W_s`, over the pairs of learned rows
`(s, s−k)`:

```
S_k = Σ_{s≥k} u_s u_{s−k} = C_k − m (Y_k + L_k) + m² P_k
P_k = Σ a_s a_{s−k}        Y_k = Σ a_s a_{s−k} y_s
L_k = Σ a_s a_{s−k} y_{s−k}    C_k = Σ a_s a_{s−k} y_s y_{s−k}
γ_k = S_k / P_0   ('mle')     or   S_k / P_k   ('adjusted')
```

`P_0 = Σ W_s` and `Y_0 = L_0 = Σ W_s y_s`. Per learned row the four arrays take
`p + 1` updates each. The ring keeps each lag's value and its current amplitude;
each row multiplies the amplitudes by `√λ` and the sums by `λ`, so a pair's
weight `a_s a_{s−k} = √(W_s W_{s−k})` stays exact across zero-weight rows. The
solve at emit is the **Levinson-Durbin recursion** on `γ_0..γ_p`: `O(p²)`,
giving `φ` and the innovation variance `σ² = γ_0 Π (1 − κ_m²)`; its guard stops
with NaN if a prediction variance is not `> 0`, or if `P_0 = 0`. `√` is
correctly rounded under IEEE 754, so these fields are bit-exact across OSes
(no libm result sits in the state).

**What matches what.** At `λ = 1` and unit weights every `a = 1`, `Y_k` is the
sum without the first `k` values and `L_k` the sum without the last `k`, and
`S_k / n` (`/ (n − k)`) is exactly statsmodels' `acovf` with `demean=True`, the
mean of the whole sample, so the fit is `yule_walker(y, order=p, method='mle'`
(or `'adjusted'`)`, demean=True)`. The mean is taken at emit from the kept sums,
not as a running mean subtracted row by row, which would not match.
`AutoReg(trend='c')` does not centre at all: it estimates `c` jointly with `φ`
by OLS on the lag design, and the two estimators differ by `O(1/N)` (they are
different estimators; each is compared with its own oracle). Yule-Walker's
implied intercept is `m (1 − Σ φ_i)`.

**Two conventions the study forced.**

1. *The pair weight.* The first form tried gave the pair `(s, s−k)` the later
   row's weight `W_s` alone. Under decay that sequence is **not positive
   definite**: at `λ = 0.999`, `p = 5`, `N = 20000` one seed of five gave a
   Toeplitz matrix with eigenvalue −0.024, and at `λ = 0.99`, `p = 20`,
   `N = 2000` three of five did, so Levinson-Durbin met a negative prediction
   variance (statsmodels' `levinson_durbin` carries on with it silently). The
   tapered form `√(W_s W_{s−k})` is the autocovariance of a finite sequence and
   so positive definite by construction, and it has a third-party oracle:
   `yule_walker(u, method='mle', demean=False)` on `u = √W (y − m)`
   (statsmodels divides by `n` rather than `Σ W`, which scales every `γ_k`
   alike and leaves `φ` unchanged).
2. *`'adjusted'` under decay.* Dividing lag `k` by its own weight `P_k` is not
   positive definite either (neither is statsmodels' `'adjusted'` in general):
   under decay it failed in 6 of 8 cells at `p ≥ 5`. So **`'mle'` is the form
   to build**; `'adjusted'` is for parity with `yule_walker(method='adjusted')`
   at no decay only, where it matched in every run.

## 3. Oracles and the test series

- **Series**: for each `p` and seed, roots of `z^p − φ_1 z^{p−1} − … − φ_p`
  drawn with moduli in `[0.3, 0.85]` (conjugate pairs, one real root when `p`
  is odd); `φ` from `numpy.poly`; checked by `numpy.roots` of `1 − Σ φ_i L^i`'s
  reciprocal polynomial (largest modulus 0.82 to 0.84 in every run).
  `y = lfilter(1, 1 − Σ φ_i L^i)(0.5 + e)`, `e ~ N(0, 1)`, 1000 rows of burn-in
  dropped. The mean `0.5 / (1 − Σ φ)` reaches 28 at `p = 20`, which is what
  makes the origin matter. Seeds 0 to 4; weights `U(0.5, 1.5)` under decay.
- **No decay**: `AutoReg(y, lags=p, trend='c').fit().params`;
  `yule_walker(y, order=p, method='mle' | 'adjusted', demean=True)` (`φ`, and
  `σ` for `'mle'`). The oracle floor is `AutoReg` against `numpy.linalg.lstsq`
  on the same design.
- **Decay** (`λ = 0.999`, `0.99`): least squares against `numpy.linalg.lstsq`
  on the rows scaled by `√(w_s λ^{age_s})` (longhand), and statsmodels `WLS`
  with those weights; Yule-Walker against the tapered autocovariances written
  out over the rows into statsmodels' `levinson_durbin(isacov=True)`, and
  against `yule_walker` on the tapered sequence.
- **Out of sample**: `pred_t` against `AutoReg` fitted on rows `< t` and
  applied at `t`, at `t = 12, 100, 399` (`p = 5`): 1.3e-14 relative.

Gaps are `max |ours − oracle|` (abs) and that over `max |oracle|` (rel), the
worst of five seeds.

### Zero-weight rows and gaps

The rule: **the lag ring advances only on a learned row.** A zero-weight row and
a missing row (a gap, the target NaN, treated as weight 0) are not lags; they
advance the clock (so they decay the state and age every lag's amplitude) and
learn nothing. This is the existing "a lag counts learned rows" rule and
`AutoReg(missing='drop')`, which closes the series up. The other reading (the
ring advances on a zero-weight row, "observed but not learned") cannot serve a
gap, which has no value to put in the ring; it is the decision the scan's
section 12 leaves open, and this study did not build it.

Test stream: the `p = 5` series of 2000 rows with 300 weight-0 rows mixed in,
half NaN and half a wild value (1e6), the first two rows of the stream among
them (a NaN, then a weight-0 123.0).

- `λ = 1`: both forms equal **to the bit** the same stream with those rows
  dropped (`β`, `G` and `φ` compared with `array_equal`).
- Least squares against `AutoReg(missing='drop')` on the series with NaN at the
  weight-0 rows: 5.7e-15 relative.
- `λ = 0.999` and `0.99`, ages counted on the clock (every row): least squares
  against the longhand WLS 6.2e-15 and 5.7e-15; Yule-Walker against
  `levinson_durbin` 1.2e-14 and 4.3e-15, and against `yule_walker` on the
  tapered sequence 1.1e-14 and 4.4e-15. No NaN anywhere in either state.
- A stream of only weight-0 rows (NaN, 1, 2 at `λ = 0.99`): `G` stays finite,
  `β` and `γ` are NaN (no fit), nothing poisoned.

A clock break would empty the ring (the existing lag rule); not exercised here.

## 5. What part 1 settles for task 486

1. Both forms are exact online at no decay and under decay, against third-party
   oracles; propose 1e-9 relative on coefficients for streams to about 2e4
   rows, as the scan proposed.
2. Build Yule-Walker in the tapered `'mle'` form; the later-row pair weight
   and `'adjusted'` under decay are not positive definite.
3. Keep the sums about an origin (the first learned value); compensated
   summation is the next step if a test needs no-decay streams much longer than
   2e4 rows at 1e-9.
4. The lag ring advances on learned rows only; zero-weight rows and gaps then
   match `AutoReg(missing='drop')` to the bit at `λ = 1`.

## 6. Spectral estimates (part 2, task 491): the question

Can a streaming spectral estimate (Welch over a ring with overlap, an
exponentially weighted periodogram, and Lomb-Scargle on an uneven clock) match
`scipy.signal` `welch`, `periodogram` and `lombscargle` to floating precision on
the same segments, and what state does it cost?

**Answer: yes, all three.** Welch and the segment periodogram sit 1e-16 to 2e-15
relative from scipy at `nperseg` 256, 1024 and 4096, `hann` and `boxcar`, overlap
0 and 50 percent; the exponentially weighted periodogram 1e-16 to 7e-16 from
scipy's per-segment `spectrogram` weighted by hand. Streaming Lomb-Scargle over
the last `W` rows sits 3e-14 (`W = 256`) to 6e-12 (`W = 4096`) relative from
`scipy.signal.lombscargle` on the window's rows, with 30 percent clock jitter or
none. scipy's `lombscargle` is astropy's `LombScargle` with the normalisations
paired below (1.4e-14 to 5.9e-14). Run: `.venv/bin/python
scripts/study_486_491.py spectral` (14 s). astropy 8.0.3 was installed into the
venv for the normalisation check only; the package's dependencies are unchanged.

### 6a. The math

**The segment periodogram.** For a segment `x_0..x_{n−1}` at sampling rate `fs`,
window `w_j` (periodic, scipy's `get_window(name, n)`), constant detrend
`x̄ = (1/n) Σ x_j`:

```
X_k  = Σ_j w_j (x_j − x̄) e^{−2πi jk/n}          k = 0..n/2   (rfft, nfft = n)
P_k  = |X_k|² / (fs Σ_j w_j²)                     two-sided density
P1_k = P_k at k = 0 and (n even) k = n/2;  2 P_k otherwise     one-sided
```

This is `periodogram(x, fs, window, detrend='constant', scaling='density')`.
The hann window written out, `0.5 − 0.5 cos(2πj/n)`, differs from scipy's
(a cosine of `linspace(−π, π)`) by one ulp (2.2e-16); the study uses scipy's
values, and a Rust build would compute the window once at construction (it is
configuration, not state).

**Welch.** Segments start at `0, h, 2h, …` with hop `h = nperseg − noverlap`; a
trailing partial segment is dropped. The estimate after `M` complete segments is
`(1/M) Σ_m P1^{(m)}`. A stream meets the segments in the same order: the ring of
the last `nperseg` rows holds segment `m` exactly when the row count is
`nperseg + m h`, so the stream computes that segment's periodogram then and adds
it to `P_sum`, `M += 1`. The read is `P_sum / M`; before the first segment it is
NaN.

**The exponentially weighted periodogram.** At each segment boundary

```
S ← β S + P1^{(m)}      Z ← β Z + 1      read S / Z
```

so segment `m`'s weight is `β^{M−1−m}`. `β` is the decay **per segment**; per row
it is `λ = β^{1/h}`, and a half-life of `H` rows is `β = 2^{−h/H}`. The sum form
never divides on update and its read is exact from the first segment (the mean
form `S ← β S + (1 − β) P` is biased towards its zero start until `β^M` is
small). Oracle:
`spectrogram(x, fs, window, nperseg, noverlap, detrend='constant',
scaling='density', mode='psd')`, whose columns are exactly Welch's segment
periodograms, combined with the weights `β^{M−1−m}` by hand.

**Lomb-Scargle on an uneven clock.** For times `t_i`, values `y_i`, weights
`p_i = w_i / Σ w` and angular frequency `ω`, with `c_i = cos ω(t_i − o)`,
`s_i = sin ω(t_i − o)`:

```
C = Σ p c   S = Σ p s   CC = Σ p c²   SS = 1 − CC   CS = Σ p c s
Y = Σ p y   YC = Σ p y c   YS = Σ p y s   YY = Σ p y²
floating_mean: CC −= C², SS −= S², CS −= C S, YC −= Y C, YS −= Y S, YY −= Y²
q  = (SS YC² − 2 CS YC YS + CC YS²) / (CC SS − CS²)        = v' M⁻¹ v
'power':     P = (N/2) q        'normalize':  P = q / YY
```

`q` is the weighted projection of `y` on `(cos, sin)` (on `(1, cos, sin)` with
`floating_mean`). scipy reaches the same number by rotating the basis by the
phase `τ`, `tan 2τ = 2 CS / (CC − SS)`, which makes the cross term zero; the
explicit 2×2 form needs no `τ`, no `arctan2` and no second pass of trig, and is
invariant to the origin `o` (the periodogram does not depend on where the clock
starts). `N` is the number of rows with `w > 0`. The six per-frequency sums
`Σw c, Σw s, Σw c², Σw c s, Σw y c, Σw y s` and three shared `Σw, Σw y, Σw y²`
are the state; `c²` and `cs` come from `c` and `s` (no extra trig).

**Which normalisation scipy matches** (astropy `method='slow'`, frequencies
`f = ω / 2π`):

| scipy `lombscargle` | astropy `LombScargle` | gap |
|---|---|---|
| `normalize='power'` (the default) | `normalization='psd', fit_mean=False, center_data=False` | 1.4e-14 |
| `normalize='normalize'` | `normalization='standard', fit_mean=False, center_data=False` | 1.4e-14 |
| `normalize='power', floating_mean=True` | `normalization='psd'`, `fit_mean=True, center_data=True` | 5.9e-14 |
| `normalize='normalize', floating_mean=True` | astropy's defaults (`standard`, `fit_mean`, `center_data`) | 5.9e-14 |
| `normalize='normalize'` on `y − ȳ` | `fit_mean=False`, `center_data=True` | 2.6e-14 |

So scipy's default `'power'` is astropy's `'psd'` (the classical Scargle power,
`½[(Σyc)²/Σc² + (Σys)²/Σs²]` in the `τ`-rotated basis, with no division by the
variance) with the mean neither fitted nor removed; astropy's default
(`'standard'`, the fraction of variance explained, with a floating mean) is
scipy's `normalize='normalize', floating_mean=True`. scipy no longer centres `y`
(`precenter` is deprecated), so its default on data with a mean puts the mean's
power at every frequency; `floating_mean=True` is the form to build.

**The streaming form.** A ring of the last `W` rows' `(t, y, w)`. A row adds its
terms to the sums; the row leaving the window subtracts its terms, its `cos` and
`sin` recomputed from the ring (storing them would cost `16 W F` bytes). Every
`W` rows the sums are rebuilt from the ring and the origin `o` is rebased to the
oldest time in it; this bounds the subtraction's drift and keeps the phase
argument `ω (t − o)` of the order of the window's span instead of the stream's.
The window counts rows on the clock: a zero-weight row holds a slot and adds
nothing.

**The Nyquist of an even clock.** At `f = fs/2` with no jitter the sine column is
`sin(π i)`, zero up to rounding, and `CC SS − CS²` is rounding noise. The read
guards it: a column whose `CC` or `SS` is under `1e-10 (CC + SS)` leaves the
one-column fit `YC²/CC` (or `YS²/SS`). scipy instead clamps `SS` at `epsneg` and
keeps `YS²/epsneg`, where `YS` is the rounding of `sin(π i)` times `y`, which
grows with `t`: scipy's own value there is off by 1e-11 (`W = 256`) to 9e-9
(`W = 4096`) relative, while the guarded stream matches the cosine-only fit
written out with `(−1)^i` to 4e-16 to 5e-14.

### 6c. Zero-weight rows

The rule: **a zero-weight row advances the clock and contributes nothing.**

- *Welch* (even clock, position matters): the row holds its slot in the ring, so
  every segment still spans `nperseg` rows of the clock. In the segment, a sample
  of weight `w_j` has amplitude `win_j √w_j` (the periodogram is quadratic in
  `x`, so `√w`, as part 1's tapered autocovariances), the constant detrend
  removes the weighted mean `Σ w x / Σ w`, and the scale is
  `1 / (fs Σ win_j² w_j)`, so a segment with missing rows is scaled up to the
  energy of the samples it has. A weight-0 sample's value is replaced by 0
  before any product (`0 · NaN` is never formed). A segment with `Σ w = 0` is not
  counted in the mean (`M` does not move) and adds nothing to `S`, but the
  exponential decay still applies at its boundary (it is on the clock). Oracle:
  `periodogram(x − m_w, fs, window=win·√w, detrend=False)` per segment, averaged
  (or weighted) by hand: 5e-17 to 6.5e-16 relative at `nperseg = 256`, both
  windows and overlaps, 10 percent weight-0 rows (half NaN), a stream opening on
  three of them, and one whole segment of them (kept 31 of 32 and 62 of 63
  segments); no NaN. At `w = 1` the weighted path equals the unweighted to the
  bit. Without data weights (all rows `w ∈ {0, 1}` or none) the weight ring is
  not needed.
- *Lomb-Scargle* (uneven clock): the row holds a slot of the window (the window
  counts rows on the clock) and adds nothing to the sums; the read's `N` counts
  rows with `w > 0`, and with none in the window the read is NaN and the sums
  are reset to exact zero (dropping the subtraction's residue). Oracle:
  `lombscargle` on the window's `w > 0` rows with `weights=w` (scipy's `'power'`
  multiplies by the number of rows it is given, so the weight-0 rows must be
  dropped, not passed with weight 0).

Operation counts. Welch: a ring write per row; per segment, `O(n)` for the
detrend and window and `O(n log n)` for the FFT, every `h` rows, so
`O((n/h) log n)` per row amortised (`2 log₂ n` at 50 percent overlap); the read
is `K` divisions. Lomb-Scargle: per row `2F` trig on entry and `2F` on leaving
(the leaving row's are recomputed) and about `12F` multiply-adds; the rebuild
`2WF` trig every `W` rows, so `2F` more trig per row amortised; the read about
`15F`. At `F = 64` that is about 384 `sin`/`cos` per row, the scan's
"sixteen frequencies cost about ten ordinary model rows" scaled by four.

### 6e. What part 2 settles for task 491, and what is open

1. Streaming Welch and the exponentially weighted periodogram are exact against
   scipy (`welch`, `periodogram`, `spectrogram`) to a few ulps; propose 1e-12
   relative. Decay is per segment, `β`; per row `β^{1/h}`.
2. Lomb-Scargle streams as six sums per frequency over a ring of `W` rows,
   matching `lombscargle` to 3e-14 to 6e-12 relative; propose 1e-10 off the
   even-clock Nyquist, and the guarded one-column read there. Build the
   `floating_mean` form; scipy's `'power'` is astropy's `'psd'` and
   `'normalize'` is astropy's `'standard'`.
3. Zero-weight rows advance the clock, hold their slot, contribute nothing;
   no NaN reaches any state.

Open questions:

- **An FFT in Rust.** The stack has none (`faer` does linear algebra). The
  candidates are a pure-Rust crate (`rustfft`/`realfft`) or a direct DFT at
  chosen bins (`O(nK)` per segment, no dependency). A new crate linked into the
  wheel and the CLI is the static-linking question that is raised before it
  lands.
- **libm in the state.** `P_sum` and `S` hold FFT results (twiddles are
  `sin`/`cos`), and the Lomb-Scargle sums hold `cos`/`sin` of the clock, so both
  fall under the cross-OS ulp rule; the window values are configuration but feed
  the same sums. Not measured here (one OS).
- **The leaving row's trig.** Recomputing it doubles the trig per row; storing
  it costs `16 W F` bytes (1 MB at `W = 1024`, `F = 64`). The recompute is the
  bounded choice; a decayed (unwindowed) Lomb-Scargle has no leaving row and
  half the trig, with `lombscargle(weights=λ^age)` as its oracle (not built).
- Not built: `detrend='linear'`, `average='median'`, `csd`/`coherence`, the
  sliding DFT at chosen bins, and the rebuild's long-run drift beyond 20000 rows.
