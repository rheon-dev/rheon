**Decisions (the note's proposals):**
- **Families:** linear-Gaussian transitions only, so the EKF and UKF prediction is exact. Observations are scalar, through one index η = z'β: the families in `family.rs`, a Gaussian with log or logit mean, and a Student t for the particle filter. Arbitrary expressions and range-bearing are refused.
- **EKF:** closed-form Jacobian only, linearised at the clipped η. Cost O(k²) per row.
- **Particle filter:** resample when ESS < 0.5·N, never on a row count. Each observing row's random stream starts from (key, n), so chunking and save/load cannot move a draw. State is 8(k+1) bytes per particle per target, capped at 256 KB per group.

**Three rulings for you:**
1. **UKF default.** The note proposes α = 1 in place of Wan and van der Merwe's 1e-3, which avoids a centre weight of about −1e6 (β = 2, κ = 0 kept). It also derives, without running it, that the full k-dimensional sigma rule's accuracy on a one-index observation depends on k and on feature order (E[e^η] off by 4.7% at k = 20). It proposes a three-point rule on η alone, O(k²), with an exact filterpy oracle. **My recommendation:** accept the proposal to build both, measure both on the study's oracles, and make the closer one the default.
2. **`libm` for `exp`, `ln` and `lgamma`.** The particle filter needs it: a 1-ulp difference can change which ancestor a resample picks. **My recommendation:** use it in all three filters for uniform bit-exactness across OSes, as `garch` does, and let part 2 measure the cost (garch measured libm's `exp` at 1.8× Apple's).
3. **Particle filter scope.** Under the 256 KB cap, N is at most 5,461 at k = 5, yet the study needed 50,000 particles at k = 5 and still saw an ESS of 176. **My recommendation:** part 2 builds the engine and the benchmark now, and the `particle` kind waits for a user model such as stochastic volatility.
